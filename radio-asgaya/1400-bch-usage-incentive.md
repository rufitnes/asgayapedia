INTRO

Welcome to Radio Asgaya. Aligning everyone's interests.

---

THE PROBLEM WITH NICE

Let's set the scene. You're a shop owner in a high-remittance neighborhood. Migrants send money back home. That's your business. Every transaction, you get a cut.

Now imagine the traditional approach to crypto adoption. Some advocate shows up and says: "You should accept Bitcoin Cash. It's faster than PayPal. It's cheaper than Western Union. Come on—just believe in the technology."

You think: that's nice. But my customers still want cash. My landlord wants cash. My suppliers want cash. And I need to eat tomorrow.

So you say no. Not because Bitcoin Cash isn't good. But because there's no incentive for you. The ideology doesn't pay your bills. The vision doesn't buy your kids dinner.

This is why a hundred blockchain projects have failed at merchant adoption. They built beautiful technology. Then they asked merchants to accept it out of goodwill.

Asgaya flips this completely. We don't ask merchants to believe. We give them a reason to want BCH.

---

THE MATH OF SELF-INTEREST

Here's how it works. When someone sends a remittance through Asgaya, the merchant gets a commission. Not a tiny amount. We're talking twenty-two to forty-four euros per transaction.

Think about what that means. A merchant who handles five remittances a week—that's one hundred to two hundred euros in extra income. Every week.

Now the conversation changes. You don't need to convince them about decentralization. You just need to show them a number.

"Your competitors down the street are earning fifty euros per day handling remittances. How much are you earning?"

That question sells itself.

The genius here is that the incentive is automatic. Every single transaction through Asgaya puts money in the merchant's pocket. Not in euros. In Bitcoin Cash. Which brings us to the next part.

---

HOLDING BCH, BECAUSE IT'S PROFITABLE

This is where it gets interesting. A merchant receives Bitcoin Cash for the first time. They think: now what? Cash it out immediately to euros?

Maybe. But here's the thing. The more merchants holding BCH in your neighborhood, the more useful that BCH becomes. Because the next person who needs to send money can come to that merchant and get cash for their covenant.

So some merchants start thinking differently. Instead of cashing out immediately, they hold. They think about their neighbors, their competitors, the ecosystem forming around them.

But this doesn't happen because we ask them to "think long term." It happens because the math works. When BCH circulates locally among merchants, every transaction stays fast, stays cheap, stays within the network.

The merchant who holds BCH becomes a node in a denser network. More senders choose their store. More transactions. More commissions.

This is the circular economy operating in real time. BCH doesn't need to touch an exchange. It doesn't need to be cashed out by every single person. It just needs to flow.

---

WHY NOT THE OTHERS?

Now someone's going to ask: why Bitcoin Cash specifically? Why not Bitcoin? Why not Ethereum? Why not just use stablecoins?

Let's take them one at a time.

Bitcoin first. Bitcoin is slow. It costs money to move. And here's the critical part: Bitcoin has no covenant support. A covenant is the smart contract that holds the remittance. That's the whole system. Bitcoin can't do it natively. You'd need complex workarounds. Asgaya needs a coin that can move money fast, cheaply, and with built-in smart contract capability.

Ethereum. Ethereum technically has smart contracts. But the gas costs destroy the economics. Remember that twenty-two to forty-four euro commission? On Ethereum, the fees to execute the covenant transaction eat half of it. Or more. The merchant's incentive disappears. The whole model breaks.

Stablecoins alone. This is important. You can't have a remittance system on stablecoins if there's no easy way to convert cash into stablecoins. That's where the seller comes in. The seller converts euros to Bitcoin Cash. But if you only used stablecoins, who's managing that conversion? You need on-ramps and off-ramps. You need infrastructure.

Asgaya provides both. The seller buys Bitcoin Cash with euros and sells it to senders. The merchant holds Bitcoin Cash and sells it back to senders for euros. The whole system creates its own market.

Bitcoin Cash has everything you need. Covenants. Speed. Cheap transactions. A global network. The math works on BCH in ways it doesn't work elsewhere.

THE ADOPTION CURVE

Here's where this gets powerful. Think about adoption spreading through a neighborhood.

Week one. You explain the system to five shops. Two of them say yes. They start handling remittances. They make good money. You're earning euros fast.

Week two. The other three shops notice. "Why is the shop next to me getting all those remittance customers?" They ask questions. You show them the commission structure.

Now four shops are in the network. Suddenly it's more useful. Senders can go to more locations. Recipients can claim from more places. The network density increases.

Week three. Five more shops want to join. Not because they believe in Bitcoin Cash. But because their competitors are making money and they're not. That's the only incentive that actually matters in retail.

This pattern scales. You don't need ideology. You don't need evangelists. You need profit. And profit is automatic in Asgaya. Every transaction creates it.

Let me tell you what actually happens inside a merchant's head.

Month one: "I handle my first remittance. I get twenty-five euros in Bitcoin Cash. That's strange. That's good. But what do I do with this?"

Month two: "I've handled fifteen remittances. That's three hundred and seventy-five euros in Bitcoin Cash. I can see the pattern now. I'm profitable."

Month six: "Bitcoin Cash circulates in my neighborhood now. Three of us handle remittances. Everyone's making money. The system works because we all profit. I'm not thinking about 'adoption' anymore. I'm thinking about expanding. Can we handle more transactions?"

That's the journey. Not from skeptic to believer. From skeptic to profitable business owner.

---

THE CIRCULAR FLOW

Let's trace what actually happens to BCH in this system.

A sender pays a BCH seller one hundred euros via their local payment method. Simple. Clean.

Behind the scenes, the seller locks one hundred seven euros worth of their own Bitcoin Cash into a covenant—one hundred euros face value plus a seven percent volatility buffer. The seller receives the sender's one hundred euros via Bizum. The fiat goes to the seller's bank account and can be used later to replenish their BCH inventory. The covenant is now live on the Bitcoin Cash blockchain, waiting for the recipient to claim.

The seller can recycle that one hundred euros to the next sender immediately. The seven euro buffer stays locked in the covenant until the recipient claims. The seller monitors the covenant and bears the volatility risk.

The recipient gets notified. They coordinate with the sender. "I can claim in two hours."

The recipient goes to a merchant within that window. The merchant validates the covenant. They confirm the amount. They hand over cash. Everyone signs. The merchant gets the Bitcoin Cash.

Now the merchant is holding Bitcoin Cash. What happens next? Maybe they hold it. Maybe they sell it back to a sender the next day. Maybe they use it to pay for goods from a wholesaler who's also in the network.

Bitcoin Cash circulates. Never touches an exchange. Never needs to. It just moves from person to person, transaction to transaction, serving the real economic purpose it was designed for: moving value.

And here's the kicker. Every time it moves, it adds value to the merchant holding it. Because each transaction is another opportunity to earn commission. Each movement makes the network denser. Denser networks have more utility. More utility creates more demand.
