# Radio Script: BCH Adoption Flywheel

**Episode 28:** Every remittance doesn't just move money. It deepens BCH adoption. Here's the cycle.

**Duration:** ~8 minutes

**Tone:** Conversational, strategic

**Target Audience:** Asgaya senders, merchants, and BCH community interested in network effects

---

Welcome to Radio Asgaya. We want you for the Asgaya army.

I'm your host, Claudia Sonnet 4.5.

Today we're talking about something that happens quietly in the background of every single remittance. It's not obvious at first. But once you see it, you can't unsee it.

Every remittance through Asgaya doesn't just move money from Spain to Venezuela. It creates a tiny domino. And that domino knocks into the next one. And that one knocks into the next. Until you have something that feeds itself.

It's called the BCH adoption flywheel. And it's the reason why Asgaya works.

---

Let me walk you through this from the beginning. Imagine you're sending money to your cousin in Caracas. One hundred euros. You find a BCH seller and pay them one hundred euros via Bizum. Behind the scenes, the seller locks one hundred seven euros worth of their own Bitcoin Cash into a covenant for your cousin—one hundred euros face value plus a seven percent volatility buffer. The seller receives your fiat and can recycle it to the next sender immediately. Their capital doesn't sit waiting. It moves.

Now the covenant is live on the blockchain. Your cousin gets notified. You coordinate: I'm sending now, can you claim in two hours? They say yes. They head to the neighborhood store—let's call it Rosa's tienda—and they claim the covenant. Rosa validates it, hands over cash, and now she's holding Bitcoin Cash.

Here's where the flywheel starts spinning.

Rosa is a shop owner. Small place. She's used to thin margins. One percent here, two percent there. But today she got Bitcoin Cash for something she was already doing—cashing out a customer. And she earned half a percent on the transaction. That's money she wasn't expecting.

Now Rosa thinks. I could do this again tomorrow. Someone else might come in with a covenant. I could cash them out, hold the BCH, and if another customer needs BCH later this week, I sell it to them at a small markup. Margin on top of my tienda margin.

But something else happens too. Rosa tells her neighbor, Miguel, who runs another shop two blocks away. Miguel overhears: You made half a percent on nothing? Just by having cash on hand at the right time? Miguel goes home thinking.

A week later, Miguel shows up at that same street corner where the BCH sellers work. He buys a small amount of Bitcoin Cash himself. He wants to be ready for covenants too.

Now you've got two merchants. Then three. Then five. And as more merchants have Bitcoin Cash on hand, something magical happens for the people sending money. If you send one hundred euros, your cousin now has options. Not just Rosa, but Miguel, and the pharmacy on the corner, and that café. Your cousin can choose the merchant with the best deal or the closest location.

For senders, choice means trust. It means more people are willing to use Asgaya. And as more senders use it, more covenants show up. More opportunities for merchants to earn. And suddenly every merchant in that neighborhood wants to participate.

This is the flywheel. And here's what makes it self-sustaining.

---

Remember, BCH never leaves the neighborhood. When a merchant receives Bitcoin Cash from a covenant, they don't sell it back to an exchange. They hold it. They recycle it. If they have a family member in Spain—a triple-dip merchant—they can sell that BCH to the next sender and earn another fee. If they're a double-dip merchant, they sell it to a local BCH buyer who provides them fiat. Either way, the BCH stays in the neighborhood, cycling through local participants. That merchant earned on one side, and they'll earn on the other side too.

You're creating a closed loop economy. Money comes in from remittances. It stays local. It cycles. It generates earnings at every touch point. And those earnings give merchants a reason to stay in the game.

The density increases. One merchant becomes five. Five becomes twenty. When you hit critical density in Caracas, suddenly expanding to Valencia becomes much easier. You already have proof of concept, trust, and merchants willing to help. The second city is built faster than the first.

And here's the thing that people miss about this model. We're not asking merchants to believe in Bitcoin Cash. We're not giving ideological speeches at street corners. We're offering economics. Merchants earn money. Right now. Today. They don't need to understand blockchain. They don't need to watch YouTube videos about crypto. They just need to see that cashing out a covenant pays better than standing behind their counter all day waiting for a customer to buy milk.

---

Compare this to what usually happens. Someone comes to a merchant and says, "Please start accepting Bitcoin Cash. It's the future. It's decentralized. It's amazing." The merchant nods politely and says no thanks. Their margin is thin. The risk is high. And they don't benefit.

Asgaya inverts that. The merchant benefits first. Economics before ideology. Earnings before evangelism. And when merchants are earning, they don't need convincing. They show up. They buy Bitcoin Cash. They hold it. They wait for covenants.

Each transaction strengthens all future transactions. Every time someone claims a covenant at Rosa's tienda, she gets better at it. She understands the process. She trusts the bulletin board. She knows her customers. And she builds reputation as the place where you can cash out fast and fair.

More reputation means more volume. More volume means more earnings. More earnings means she recruits her friend Miguel more aggressively. He sees her success and stops hesitating.

This compounds. And that compounding is what you can't buy with subsidies.

---

You can't pay a merchant enough to stay interested in Bitcoin Cash indefinitely. But you can align their incentives with the network's growth. You do that by making every transaction they facilitate earn them money. You do that by creating density so there's choice. You do that by keeping Bitcoin Cash local so the liquidity stays concentrated instead of dispersing across exchanges.

The end state is a self-sustaining merchant network. No subsidies. No evangelism. No begging for adoption. Just merchants doing what merchants do: earning.

And senders getting reliable, fast, cheap remittances home.

---

That's the BCH adoption flywheel. It runs on self-interest. It compounds with each transaction. It sustains itself through density and local circulation.

Every remittance through Asgaya isn't just moving money. It's deepening the network. It's recruiting the next merchant. It's proving to the next city that this works.

That's what a flywheel is. Small force applied consistently. Spinning faster each time. Until it's unstoppable.

Thanks for listening. This is Radio Asgaya.
