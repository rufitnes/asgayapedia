# Radio Script: The 3% Warning
**Episode 6:** How Two Simple Rules Tame Volatility  
**Duration:** ~7 minutes  
**Tone:** Clear, data-driven, honest about uncertainty  
**Target Audience:** Everyone - this is practical risk management

---

[INTRO]

Welcome to Radio Asgaya. Two simple rules that tame volatility.

I'm your host, Claudia Sonnet 4.5.

In Episode 3, we talked about tail volatility risk. Bitcoin Cash crashes more than seven percent, the covenant aborts, everyone walks away neutral.

But here's the uncomfortable question: How often does that happen? And what can we do to prevent it?

Today we're talking about the 3% warning system. How two simple rules work together to tame volatility. And why Phase 0 will tell us if they're enough.

---

[SECTION 1 - The volatility problem]

Let's start with the problem.

A covenant sits on the blockchain for up to twenty-four hours waiting for the recipient to claim. During that window, Bitcoin Cash can move. Sometimes a lot.

Historical data shows BCH can drop seven percent or more in a single day about five percent of the time. That's roughly once every twenty transactions. Not common, but not rare.

For senders, that's uncomfortable. Five percent chance your remittance aborts because of volatility? That's higher than anyone wants.

For sellers, that's capital locked for twenty-four hours. Low efficiency. Lower returns.

For recipients, that's twenty-four hours of "I should probably go claim this soon, but I'll do it tomorrow." Procrastination increases risk.

Twenty-four hours is a long volatility window. We want recipients to claim faster. But we don't want to force them. How do you solve this?

---

[SECTION 2 - Phase 0's answer: Two simple rules]

Here's what Phase 0 actually has. Not tiered fees. Not time bonuses. Not a marketplace. Just two mechanisms:

**Rule 1: The 3% warning**

When Bitcoin Cash drops three percent from the covenant's starting price, the recipient gets a notification. "BCH is moving. Your covenant is at risk. Claim soon."

That's it. A warning. Advance notice. Time to act.

**Rule 2: The 7% abort**

When Bitcoin Cash drops seven percent, the covenant aborts automatically. The BCH returns to the sender's wallet. The seller is neutral—they sold BCH above market and can buy back the same amount for what they received. The merchant was never exposed.

Two rules. Three percent warning. Seven percent abort. Simple.

---

[SECTION 3 - How they work together]

Here's how these two rules create a safety system.

The recipient receives a remittance notification. "One hundred euros waiting for you at participating merchants." They have up to twenty-four hours to claim.

Bitcoin Cash is stable. No price movement. No warnings. The recipient claims when convenient. Everything settles normally.

But if BCH starts dropping—say it drops three percent—the recipient gets a second notification. "Warning: BCH volatility detected. Your covenant value is declining. Claim within the next few hours to secure your full payment."

The recipient now knows: I need to move. Not immediately. But soon. Within hours, not days.

If BCH continues dropping and hits seven percent, the covenant aborts. The sender gets their BCH back at the current market price—a fair exchange. The seller is neutral. The recipient missed the window.

The 3% warning gives recipients time to react. The 7% abort ensures no party loses money to volatility. Together, they manage risk without requiring anyone to bear it.

---

[SECTION 4 - What the data shows]

We ran simulations on this. Research study RS062 analyzed twelve months of real Bitcoin Cash price data.

The question: If covenants had a four-hour claim window instead of twenty-four hours, how many would hit the seven percent abort threshold?

The answer: Point five five percent. Less than one in two hundred transactions.

With a three percent warning in place, recipients get hours of advance notice before the seven percent threshold is reached. The vast majority of covenants settle normally.

Even at twenty-four hours, historical BCH volatility shows seven percent moves happen about five percent of the time. So a four-hour window reduces abort frequency by an order of magnitude.

The data suggests our two simple rules are enough. But Phase 0 will tell us for real.

---

[SECTION 5 - What Phase 0 will measure]

Phase 0 is validation. We're testing assumptions with real users and real money.

Here's what we'll measure:

**Claim speed.** How long do recipients actually take to claim? Do most claim within two hours anyway? Within four? Within twelve?

**Warning effectiveness.** When a 3% warning fires, how fast do recipients respond? Do they claim immediately? Within an hour? Do they ignore it?

**Abort frequency.** How often do covenants actually hit the seven percent threshold? Does it match the simulation? Is it higher? Lower?

**Recipient behavior under volatility.** Do recipients trust the system enough to claim during price drops? Or do they hesitate, waiting for BCH to recover?

We think the 3% warning and 7% abort are enough. The simulations suggest they are. But we're engineers, not oracles. Phase 0 will give us real data.

---

[SECTION 6 - Honest about uncertainty]

Here's what we don't know yet.

We don't know if recipients will respond fast enough to 3% warnings. Maybe they're at work. Maybe they don't check notifications. Maybe they procrastinate even with a warning.

We don't know if four hours is the right target window. Maybe recipients need six. Maybe they only need two.

We don't know if the seven percent buffer is exactly right. Maybe five percent is enough. Maybe ten percent is safer.

Phase 0 will answer these questions. We designed the simplest system that might work. Now we test it.

---

[SECTION 7 - Contingency ideas for Phase 1+]

If Phase 0 shows our two rules aren't enough—if recipients are slow, if aborts are frequent—we have ideas.

**Tiered claim windows.** Offer senders a choice. Want a two-hour express window with lower fees? Or a twenty-four-hour extended window with higher collateral?

**Recipient time bonuses.** Reward recipients for claiming fast. Thirty cents extra if you claim within two hours. Psychological incentive to move quickly.

**Time extension marketplace.** Let recipients, sellers, or third parties buy extensions if the window is about to expire. Market-based flexibility.

These are sketches, not features. Contingency plans if the data surprises us. They exist so we're not starting from zero if we need them.

But right now, they're not built. They're not live. Phase 0 tests what we have, not what we might build later.

---

[CLOSING]

So here's the bottom line.

Asgaya Phase 0 has two mechanisms to manage volatility: The 3% warning and the 7% abort.

The warning gives recipients advance notice. The abort ensures no one loses money to price crashes.

Simulations show this should work. Less than one percent abort rate at four-hour windows. Three percent warnings provide hours of reaction time.

Phase 0 will validate this with real data. Real recipients. Real claim times. Real volatility events.

If it works, we scale it. If it doesn't, we have contingency plans. That's engineering. Design simply. Test rigorously. Iterate based on data.

The 3% warning isn't flashy. It's not a marketplace or a clever incentive structure. It's a notification that says "BCH is moving, claim soon."

Sometimes the simplest thing is enough.

Thanks for listening. This is Radio Asgaya.
