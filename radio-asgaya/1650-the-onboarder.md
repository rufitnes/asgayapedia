# Radio Script: The Onboarder — How Trust Becomes Inventory

**Episode 15.5:** You don't need bank accounts in two countries. You need trust in one neighborhood.

**Duration:** ~13 minutes

**Tone:** Strategic, relationship-focused, practical

**Target Audience:** Local community builders, potential merchant recruiters, people without dual citizenship

---

[INTRO]

Welcome to Radio Asgaya. Building networks, one handshake at a time.

I'm your host, Claudia Sonnet 4.5.

Last episode, we talked about the 1% arbitrage. Dual citizens with bank accounts in two countries buying Bitcoin Cash cheap in Venezuela and selling it expensive in Spain. Cross-border market-making. Pure spread capture.

Beautiful model. But it requires dual banking. Dual payment methods. Capital to deploy across two countries.

What if you don't have that? What if you're just someone living in Venezuela? Or just someone living in Spain? No dual citizenship. No bank accounts in two countries. Just you, in one place, trying to figure out how to participate in this system.

Can you still become a BCH buyer? Can you still build inventory and eventually become a seller?

Yes. But the path is different.

Today we're talking about onboarders. The people who recruit merchants. The people who build trust. The people who do the hard, unglamorous work of growing the network—and get rewarded for it.

This is the local path. No arbitrage. No cross-border complexity. Just relationships. And those relationships turn into capital.

---

[SECTION 1 - The Merchant Liquidity Problem]

Let's start with a problem.

You're a merchant in Caracas. You've been handling Asgaya remittances for three months. You're profitable. You're earning half a percent on every transaction you validate. You're also earning product margins when recipients buy from your store.

But here's the thing: you're accumulating Bitcoin Cash.

Every time you validate a covenant and hand over cash to a recipient, you receive BCH. After three months, you might have five hundred euros worth of Bitcoin Cash in your wallet.

That's great. But you can't pay your rent in BCH. You can't pay your suppliers in BCH. At some point, you need to convert some of that Bitcoin Cash back to bolívares. Back to local currency.

What are your options?

Option one: Use a centralized exchange. Sign up. Go through KYC. Wait for verification. Deposit your BCH. Sell it. Withdraw bolívares to your bank account. Wait three days for settlement. Pay withdrawal fees. Pay exchange fees. Deal with the hassle.

Option two: Find a local buyer. Someone you trust. Someone who wants Bitcoin Cash and is willing to pay you in bolívares. Cash. Immediate. No fees. No KYC. No waiting.

Which would you prefer?

Obviously option two. But here's the problem: where do you find that local buyer? How do you know they're trustworthy? How do you know they'll give you a fair price?

This is the merchant liquidity problem. Merchants earn BCH but need fiat. They need someone reliable to sell to.

---

[SECTION 2 - The Onboarder Solution]

Now imagine you're the person who recruited that merchant.

Three months ago, you walked into their shop. You explained Asgaya. You showed them how covenant validation works. You answered their questions. You helped them set up their wallet. You walked them through their first transaction.

You did the work. You built the relationship. You earned their trust.

Now that merchant has five hundred euros worth of BCH and wants to sell some of it. Who are they going to call?

You.

Not some stranger from the internet. Not an exchange they've never heard of. You. The person who onboarded them. The person who helped them. The person they trust.

You're their liquidity provider. Their first-call buyer.

And here's the beautiful part: because you onboarded them, you get preferential terms. They're not going to charge you a premium. They might even give you a small discount. Because you're not just a buyer. You're their partner. You're the reason they're making money with Asgaya in the first place.

This is the onboarder model. You recruit merchants. You build trust. And that trust turns into buying privilege.

---

[SECTION 3 - The Economics of Onboarding]

Let's talk about the numbers.

You onboard five merchants in your neighborhood. Small shops. Each one handles maybe twenty to thirty remittances a month. Each merchant earns half a percent on each transaction. Over three months, each merchant accumulates maybe three hundred to five hundred euros worth of Bitcoin Cash.

Not all of them want to sell. Some merchants hold. They're believers. They want BCH exposure. Fine.

But let's say two out of five want to convert some of their BCH to fiat every month. They each want to sell two hundred euros worth.

That's four hundred euros worth of Bitcoin Cash available to you every month. To buy. From people you know. At fair prices.

Now here's the key: you're not paying market rate. You're paying maybe minus quarter percent. A small discount. Because you onboarded them. Because you're providing instant liquidity. Because they trust you and want to keep the relationship good.

You buy four hundred euros worth of BCH at minus quarter percent. But here's the crucial part: you don't accumulate this as savings. You turn it over immediately. The same day or the next day, you resell it at market rate or slightly above.

Where do you sell it? To senders in your city. Or to arbitrageurs who need BCH inventory. Or on the bulletin board to anyone willing to pay your asking price. You're not holding it for three months. You're flipping it in days.

Now what's your profit model?

---

[SECTION 4 - From Buyer to Seller]

Here's where the path opens up.

You buy four hundred euros worth of BCH this week from your merchants at minus quarter percent. You paid three hundred ninety-nine euros. Now you immediately list yourself on the bulletin board as a BCH seller.

A sender in your city wants to send one hundred euros to Venezuela. They find you on the bulletin board. You're listed as a BCH seller. You accept local payment—Bizum in Spain, PagoMóvil in Venezuela, whatever the local rail is.

The sender pays you one hundred euros at market rate. You lock one hundred seven euros worth of your BCH into a covenant. You just sold at market rate what you bought at minus quarter percent. That's a quarter percent profit on one hundred euros. Plus you earn the seller's half percent fee. Total: three quarters of a percent.

The sender's one hundred euros goes to your bank account. Next week, you use that same one hundred euros to buy more BCH from your merchants. The cycle continues.

You're not accumulating savings. You're running a weekly arbitrage loop. Buy from merchants at discount Monday through Wednesday. Sell to senders at market rate Thursday through Saturday. Use the proceeds to buy again next week.

This is the path from onboarder to buyer to seller. You don't need dual citizenship. You don't need cross-border arbitrage. You don't need to save up capital for months. You just need to recruit merchants, build trust, and turn that trust into a weekly buying opportunity with immediate resale.

---

[SECTION 5 - Why Onboarding Is Hard]

Now let's be honest. Onboarding merchants is not easy.

You have to find them. You have to explain a complex system. You have to overcome skepticism. You have to answer questions you might not know the answer to. You have to walk them through setup. You have to be available when things go wrong.

This is not passive income. This is work.

And here's the thing: most people won't do it. Most people want to buy Bitcoin Cash on an exchange and click a button. Most people don't want to walk into a shop, build a relationship, recruit someone, and support them for months.

But that's exactly why it's valuable.

Because most people won't do it, the people who do get rewarded. The reward is not a fee. The reward is access. Access to a liquidity source that other buyers don't have. Access to BCH at a discount. Access to a relationship that creates recurring buying opportunities.

This is not scalable through automation. You can't recruit a merchant with a script. You can't build trust with an app. You need face-to-face. You need language. You need culture. You need to be part of the community.

That's the moat. That's why onboarders get preferential terms. Because what they provide can't be replicated by someone sitting at a computer in another country.

---

[SECTION 6 - The Phase Zero Criticality]

Let's zoom out and talk about why this matters for Asgaya as a whole.

Phase Zero needs five merchants. Who recruits them?

Suso recruits merchant one. Maybe merchant two. He has family connections. He can make introductions.

But merchant three, four, five? Someone else needs to do that work. Someone local. Someone who knows the neighborhood. Someone who can explain the system in the local context.

That someone is an onboarder.

Without onboarders, merchant recruitment doesn't scale. Suso can't be everywhere. He can't onboard every merchant in every neighborhood in every city. He needs people on the ground doing the work.

And those people need an incentive. The incentive is not a salary. It's not a bounty. It's the buying privilege. The right to be the first-call liquidity provider for the merchants they recruit.

This aligns incentives perfectly. The onboarder wants merchants to succeed because successful merchants accumulate more BCH, which means more buying opportunities. The merchant wants the onboarder to succeed because the onboarder is their reliable buyer.

Symbiotic. Not transactional.

---

[SECTION 7 - Local vs. Cross-Border]

Let's contrast the onboarder with the arbitrageur we talked about last episode.

The arbitrageur operates cross-border. They buy BCH in Venezuela at minus half percent. They sell it in Spain at plus half percent. They earn the 1% spread. They're market makers. They're moving BCH from where it's abundant to where it's scarce.

The onboarder operates locally. They buy BCH from merchants in their own city. They might sell it to senders in the same city. Or they might hold it. Or they might sell it to an arbitrageur. They're relationship builders. They're providing liquidity where trust exists.

Both roles are essential.

The arbitrageur keeps spreads tight across borders. The onboarder keeps merchants supplied with liquidity locally.

The arbitrageur needs capital and dual banking. The onboarder needs time and trust.

The arbitrageur earns from spread capture. The onboarder earns from volume and turnover.

They complement each other. They're not competing. They're serving different parts of the ecosystem.

And here's the interesting part: an onboarder in Venezuela might sell their accumulated BCH to an arbitrageur. The arbitrageur takes that BCH to Spain and sells it to senders. The senders create covenants. Recipients in Venezuela claim from merchants. Those merchants accumulate BCH. The onboarder buys it from them. The cycle continues.

The onboarder and the arbitrageur are both moving BCH through the system. Just at different scales and in different directions.

---

[SECTION 8 - The Trust Premium]

Let's talk about something we haven't addressed yet: why would a merchant give you a discount?

You onboarded them. Great. But they could still just sell their BCH on an exchange at market rate. Or sell it to someone else willing to pay full price. Why give you preferential terms?

Three reasons.

First: Convenience. You're local. You're available. You can meet them in person. You can pay in cash or local transfer. Instant. No waiting. No exchange verification. No withdrawal delays. That convenience is worth a quarter percent.

Second: Trust. They know you. They know you're not going to scam them. They know you'll show up. They know you'll pay what you promised. Trust reduces risk. Risk reduction is worth a quarter percent.

Third: Relationship. You helped them get started. You answered their questions. You were there when they needed support. They want to maintain that relationship. They want to keep you as their buyer because they know you'll be there next month when they have more BCH to sell. Loyalty is worth a quarter percent.

This is not charity. This is rational economics. The merchant is optimizing for more than just price. They're optimizing for reliability, speed, and relationship quality.

And you, the onboarder, are providing something the market can't: personalized, local, trusted liquidity.

---

[SECTION 9 - How to Become an Onboarder]

So how do you actually do this?

Step one: Learn the system. You can't onboard someone if you don't understand how covenants work, how validation works, how the bulletin board works. You need to be the expert. Study. Practice. Run a test transaction yourself.

Step two: Identify merchants. Look for small shops in neighborhoods with high remittance traffic. Grocery stores. Pharmacies. Corner shops. Places that already handle cash. Places where the owner is present and makes decisions.

Step three: Make the pitch. Walk in. Introduce yourself. Explain that you have a way for them to earn extra income by handling remittances. Don't oversell. Be honest about the work involved. Explain the half-percent fee. Explain the foot traffic. Explain the product sales opportunity.

Step four: Support them. Help them set up. Walk them through their first transaction. Be available when they have questions. Follow up. Check in. Build the relationship.

Step five: Be their buyer. When they accumulate BCH and want to convert some to fiat, be ready. Offer fair terms. Pay promptly. Be reliable. Prove that you're not just a recruiter—you're a partner.

Repeat. Five merchants. Ten merchants. Fifteen merchants. Each one is a liquidity source. Each one is a buying opportunity.

Over time, you're not just an onboarder. You're a local BCH market maker. You're the person people call when they need to buy or sell Bitcoin Cash in your area.

Trust becomes inventory. Inventory becomes capital. Capital becomes opportunity.

If you're in Venezuela, in Spain, in any city where Asgaya operates—if you know your neighborhood, if you can build relationships, if you're willing to do the work that most people won't—this path is yours.

Start with one merchant. Prove it works. Build from there.
