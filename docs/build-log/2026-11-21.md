# Day 9 — 2026-11-21

**Shipped**
- **The first mainnet sale.** A real **€5 Bizum** to the merchant's phone became **0.02759381 BCH** in the buyer's **Selene** wallet, on Bitcoin Cash mainnet: `fa70405df39321b6cd5f03e8dab3ffbd848e85c512212d2edb929fc75938958d`. Earlier that day, two €0.50 sales (`27803203…`, `0039ac34…`) proved the loop.
- **The judge-like flow**: Mercar in a desktop browser, Selene on the phone by WizardConnect (QR), the merchant's own phone reading the bank notification, quoting at the rate of receipt (fee 0) and paying BCH.
- **AsgayaHusk on mainnet**: mainnet Electrum, the merchant wallet on its mainnet address, a 2 sat/B direct payout fee, and a hard rule that a UTXO carrying the identity NFT is never spent as plain BCH.
- **The receipt in the chat**: after the payment the merchant sends `[PAY_RECEIVED]`; the web now re-subscribes when the tab regains focus, so the confirmation is not lost when the buyer switches to the bank app.
- **The identity anchor, on-device**: the ad carries `anchor=<txid>:0` and Mercar verifies it on-chain; editing an ad now carries the anchor over instead of dropping it.

**Verified**
- The buyer's wallet holds the BCH from the quoted transaction; the merchant keeps the anchor NFT and the change.
- The quote fixes the **BCH amount**; the wallet's EUR valuation is the wallet's own feed (Selene showed €4.95 for the €5 purchase). We are explicit about the quote.
- The merchant/buyer roles follow the **bank notification**: the phone whose bank posts the push runs the merchant app.

**Decisions**
- The merchant is the device with the working bank notification; the BCH key is on-chain, so the app follows the key, not the hardware.
- The direct payout uses the public EUR/BCH feed (`oracle.asgaya.org/quote`, Kraken BCHEUR) for the merchant's quote; the signed oracle stays the covenant's piece.

**Next**

**One lesson**
- The bank notification is the settlement trigger — a bank app that stays silent is a product bug, not an edge case.
