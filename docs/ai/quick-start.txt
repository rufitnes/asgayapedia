WHAT IS ASGAYA?
==============================================

Asgaya is a permissionless, peer-to-peer remittance protocol that enables cross-border
money transfers using Bitcoin Cash covenant smart contracts, with no custody, no
intermediation, and no KYC requirements.

**Tagline:** Bitcoin Cash remittances with <1% fees, no KYC, self-custody

**Key Innovation:** Covenant-based pull system eliminates volatility risk while
maintaining regulatory compliance (no CASP/PSD2 licensing needed under MiCA)


CORE REQUIREMENTS
------------------

Asgaya satisfies three fundamental requirements:

1. CHEAPER THAN LEGACY (<1% fees)
   - Traditional remittances: 6.49% average cost (World Bank)
   - Asgaya target: 1% total fee (sender pays)
   - On €100 transfer: Save €5.49 vs. Western Union/MoneyGram
   - Enabled by: No Kraken purchase, no custody overhead, peer-to-peer rails

2. PROMOTE BCH ADOPTION
   - Every transaction creates economic incentives for BCH accumulation
   - Merchants earn 0.5% per transaction in BCH
   - BCH sellers earn 0.5% + hedge benefits
   - Self-sustaining circular economy emerges naturally

3. PERMISSIONLESS
   - No KYC requirements (peer-to-peer coordination)
   - Minimal hardware (Android phone sufficient)
   - Minimal knowledge (if you can use WhatsApp, you can use Asgaya)
   - Self-custody (users control their BCH via covenant smart contracts)


FEE STRUCTURE (Updated May 11, 2026)
-------------------------------------

Total fee: 1% of transfer amount (sender pays)

On a €100 transfer, the 1% (€1.00) is distributed as:
- **BCH Seller:** €0.50 (0.5%) - Posts overcollateralized BCH, earns fixed fee
- **Merchant:** €0.50 (0.5%) - Hands cash to recipient, receives BCH
- **Protocol:** €0.00 (0%) - No protocol fee = pure bulletin board (regulatory compliance)

Comparison: €1.00 (Asgaya) vs €6.49 (traditional) = **6.5× cheaper**

**Key change from v1.0:** No escrow operator, no Kraken exchange fee, no liquidity provider.
The covenant architecture eliminates these intermediaries entirely.

THE COVENANT MODEL (Updated May 10, 2026)
------------------------------------------

Asgaya uses **Bitcoin Cash CashScript covenants** (smart contracts) to enable trustless,
autonomous settlement without any party holding custody of funds.

**What is a covenant?**
A covenant is a Bitcoin Cash smart contract that:
- Holds BCH in a contract address (not controlled by any single party)
- Specifies conditions for fund distribution
- Executes autonomously when conditions are met (cryptographic signatures)
- Cannot be overridden by any human (immutable once created)

**Why covenants solve the regulatory problem:**
- No custody (BCH seller posts their own BCH, not held by Asgaya) ✅
- No intermediation (covenant executes autonomously, no entity controls distribution) ✅
- No conditional transfers by Asgaya (just bulletin board coordination) ✅
- Information society service (pure bulletin board = MiCA/PSD2 exempt) ✅


HOW THE COVENANT FLOW WORKS
----------------------------

**Phase 1: Covenant Creation & Funding (5 minutes)**

1. **Sender (Iris in Spain) creates covenant:**
   - Amount: €100 worth of BCH (calculated at maturity rate, not creation rate)
   - Recipient: Elena (Venezuela)
   - Payment method: Bizum (Spanish mobile payment)
   - Timeout: 24 hours (if not executed, automatic refund)
   - Published to bulletin board as "open bounty"

2. **BCH seller (bot monitoring bulletin board) accepts:**
   - Posts overcollateralized BCH: €107 worth (7% buffer for volatility protection)
   - Provides bank details for Bizum payment
   - 5-minute window for Iris to send Bizum

3. **Iris pays BCH seller:**
   - Sends €100 Bizum to seller's phone number
   - Seller bot parses SMS notification
   - Seller signs covenant: "Condition 1 satisfied - Payment received"

4. **Elena notified:**
   - SMS: "Iris sent you €100, claim at associated merchant"
   - Display: "500,000 VES available" (using DolarAPI blue dollar rate)
   - 24-hour claim window starts


**Phase 2: Recipient-Triggered Execution (30 seconds - 24 hours)**

1. **Elena goes to merchant (whenever she's ready):**
   - Shows bounty code: "8473" (4-digit code from notification)
   - Merchant sees: "Sell 500,000 VES, receive €99.50 BCH"

2. **Merchant accepts:**
   - Counts 500,000 VES cash carefully
   - Hands ALL cash to Elena FIRST (critical: cash before signature)

3. **Both co-sign covenant:**
   - Elena: "I received the cash" (cryptographic signature)
   - Merchant: "I handed the cash" (cryptographic signature)
   - **Covenant executes automatically when both conditions met**

4. **Automatic distribution:**
   - Merchant receives: €99.50 worth of BCH (at current spot rate)
   - BCH seller receives: €7.50 worth of BCH (overcollateralization surplus + fee)


**Phase 3: Timeout Handling (If No Execution)**

If Elena doesn't cash out within 24 hours:
- Covenant automatically refunds:
  - Merchant portion (€99.50 BCH) → Iris's refund address
  - Seller portion (€7.50 BCH) → BCH seller
- Iris gets her money back (in BCH, not EUR)
- Seller keeps €100 Bizum + €7.50 BCH (earned fee for service + risk)
- Elena loses nothing (never engaged)


==============================================
THE PULL SYSTEM
==============================================

KEY INNOVATION: Recipient Controls WHEN Execution Happens
----------------------------------------------------------

Traditional push-based remittances:
❌ Sender initiates → Funds move immediately → Recipient claims ASAP
❌ Volatility window = entire claim period (hours to days)
❌ Timezone coordination required (sender and recipient must be available)
❌ Recipient has no control over timing

Previous crypto attempts (2-step escrow):
❌ Sender pays escrow → Escrow holds EUR → Recipient claims → Escrow buys BCH
❌ Still requires recipient to claim within volatility window
❌ Escrow custody = CASP licensing required under MiCA

**Asgaya covenant pull system:**
✅ Sender creates covenant → Funds locked in smart contract → Recipient claims WHENEVER
✅ Volatility window = 30 seconds (only when recipient actually cashes out)
✅ No timezone coordination (recipient goes to merchant when convenient)
✅ Recipient has full control over execution timing


WHY THIS MATTERS (Venezuela Context)
-------------------------------------

Elena in Venezuela faces:
- Unreliable power (outages common)
- Unpredictable work schedule
- Distance to merchant (might be 30 min walk)
- Need to coordinate with merchant availability

**Pull system allows:**
- Elena can claim Monday morning (power reliable, she's free)
- Elena can claim Saturday afternoon (when merchant is open)
- Elena can claim whenever convenient (no rush, no coordination)
- **24-hour window gives flexibility without volatility risk**

**Covenant waits patiently. No pressure. No volatility exposure during wait.**


HOW VOLATILITY PROTECTION WORKS
--------------------------------

The covenant is **EUR-denominated** (futures contract):

**NOT:** "Merchant gets 0.0995 BCH" (fixed BCH amount)
**YES:** "Merchant gets €99.50 worth of BCH" (fixed EUR value, calculated at maturity)

Example:
- Covenant created: 1 BCH = €1,000 (estimated 0.1 BCH needed)
- BCH seller posts: 0.107 BCH (€107 at creation)
- Elena claims 12 hours later: 1 BCH = €950 (price dropped 5%)
- Merchant receives: €99.50 / €950 = **0.1047 BCH** (more BCH to reach €99.50 value)
- Seller receives: 0.107 - 0.1047 = **0.0023 BCH** (worth €2.19 at maturity)

**Merchant got full €99.50 value (protected from drop)**
**Seller absorbed the loss (that's what overcollateralization is for)**

WHO ARE BCH SELLERS?
--------------------

BCH sellers are entities that:

- Post overcollateralized BCH to covenant contracts

**Ideal sellers: BCH miners**

- Want to convert some BCH → EUR for operational costs (electricity, hardware)
- Covenant provides **partial hedge** (better than selling on exchange)


THE SELLER HEDGE MECHANISM (Discovered May 11, 2026)
-----------------------------------------------------

**Key insight:** BCH sellers don't just earn a fee - they get a **built-in hedge** that
makes them BETTER OFF than just holding BCH in BOTH price directions.

**How the hedge works:**

Traditional BCH holder:
- Owns 0.107 BCH
- Fully exposed to price volatility
- If price drops 5% → Loses €5.35
- If price rises 5% → Gains €5.35

Covenant BCH seller:
- Posts 0.107 BCH to covenant
- Receives €100 fiat IMMEDIATELY (within 5 minutes, before price moves)
- Only ~0.007 BCH remains exposed to volatility (the surplus)
- **94% of value converted to stable fiat upfront**

**Example: BCH drops 5% (€1,000 → €950)**

Just holding 0.107 BCH:
- Value at maturity: 0.107 × €950 = €101.65
- Loss: €5.35

Covenant seller:
- €100 Bizum (received before drop, locked in at old price)
- 0.0023 BCH surplus (worth €2.19 at new price)
- Total: €100 + €2.19 = **€102.19**
- Better than holding by: €102.19 - €101.65 = **€0.54**

**Example: BCH rises 5% (€1,000 → €1,050)**

Just holding 0.107 BCH:
- Value at maturity: 0.107 × €1,050 = €112.35
- Gain: €5.35

Covenant seller:
- €100 Bizum (locked in, can't benefit from rise on this portion)
- 0.0122 BCH surplus (worth €12.81 at new price)
- Total: €100 + €12.81 = **€112.81**
- Better than holding by: €112.81 - €112.35 = **€0.46**

**RESULT: Seller wins in BOTH directions!** 🎯
- Price drops → Better by €0.54 (hedge protected fiat portion)
- Price rises → Better by €0.46 (surplus amplifies gains)
- Plus: Always earn €0.50 fixed fee regardless of price movement

OVERCOLLATERALIZATION EXPLAINED
==============================================

WHY 7% BUFFER?
--------------

BCH is volatile. During the 24-hour claim window, price can move:
- Typical daily volatility: 2-3%
- Moderate volatility: 5-7%
- Extreme volatility: 10-15% (rare but happens)

**If merchant needs €99.50 and BCH drops 8%, where does the shortfall come from?**

Answer: The seller's overcollateralization buffer.

**Example:**
- Covenant created: 1 BCH = €1,000
- Seller posts: 0.107 BCH (€107)
- Merchant should get: €99.50 worth

BCH drops 8% (€1,000 → €920):
- Without overcollateralization:
  - 0.1 BCH × €920 = €92 (merchant shorted €7.50!)
- With 7% overcollateralization:
  - Merchant needs: €99.50 / €920 = 0.1082 BCH
  - Seller posted: 0.107 BCH
  - Surplus: 0.107 - 0.1082 = -0.0012 BCH (INSUFFICIENT!)
  - **Margin call triggered** (seller must add more BCH within 60 min)

**The 7% buffer covers typical volatility but not extreme swings.**


MARGIN CALL MECHANISM
----------------------

If BCH price drops >7% during covenant lifetime:
1. Seller bot detects price drop (monitors BCH/EUR spot price)
2. Calculates shortfall: How much more BCH needed to fulfill €99.50 promise?
3. Sends notification: SMS + app push (5-min lag acceptable)
4. Seller has 60 minutes to add BCH to covenant
5. If seller adds BCH → Covenant remains valid
6. If seller doesn't respond → Covenant auto-refunds after 60 min grace period

**Known risk:** Seller might be:
- Asleep (overnight price crash)
- Offline (no internet connection)
- Unable to respond (traveling, emergency)

**Mitigation:** Phase 0 will validate whether 7% is sufficient or if 10-15% needed.


PHASE 0 VALIDATION (Bitcoin Block Size Lesson)
-----------------------------------------------

The 7% rate is a **hypothesis to be validated**, not a fixed permanent parameter.

**Why this matters:**
- Bitcoin's 1MB block size seemed reasonable in 2010
- By 2017, it caused network congestion and $50 transaction fees
- Arbitrary limit without empirical validation = future problem

**Asgaya approach:**
- Phase 0 trial: 50-100 transactions over 30 days
- Track: How often does price move >7%? Margin call frequency?
- Adjust: If >10% of transactions trigger margin calls, increase to 10% or 15%
- Document: Phase 0 Validation Checklist tracks all arbitrary parameters

KEY ARCHITECTURAL CHANGES (May 10, 2026 Pivot)
==============================================

WHAT CHANGED FROM VERSION 1.0
------------------------------

The Asgaya architecture underwent a fundamental pivot on May 10, 2026, driven by
regulatory research (RS052) that identified the escrow-based model as non-compliant
with MiCA/PSD2 regulations.

**OLD Architecture (Escrow-based, pre-May 10):**

Flow:
1. Sender pays escrow (EUR in custody)
2. Escrow holds EUR temporarily
3. Recipient claims at merchant
4. Merchant confirms cash handover
5. Escrow buys BCH on Kraken
6. Escrow sends BCH to merchant
7. LP (liquidity provider) sends fiat to merchant (instant settlement option)

Fee split (three-way):
- Escrow operator: ~€0.247 (0.247%)
- Merchant: ~€0.247 (0.247%)
- Liquidity Provider: ~€0.247 (0.247%)
- Kraken exchange fee: ~€0.26 (deducted first)

Regulatory issues:
❌ Escrow holds EUR in custody (CASP licensing required under MiCA)
❌ Escrow performs intermediation (PSD2 payment service)
❌ Conditional transfer by central entity (Asgaya controls distribution)
❌ Requires Kraken account (KYC for escrow, not sender)

**NEW Architecture (Covenant-based, May 10+):**

Flow:
1. Sender creates covenant (no custody, just smart contract)
2. BCH seller posts overcollateralized BCH (seller's own funds)
3. Sender pays BCH seller via Bizum (peer-to-peer, no custody)
4. Recipient claims at merchant whenever ready
5. Merchant and recipient co-sign covenant
6. Covenant executes autonomously (no entity controls distribution)
7. Merchant receives BCH, seller receives surplus

Fee split (two-way):
- BCH Seller: €0.50 (0.5%)
- Merchant: €0.50 (0.5%)
- Protocol: €0.00 (0% - no protocol fee = pure bulletin board)

Regulatory status:
✅ No custody (seller posts own BCH, not held by Asgaya)
✅ No intermediation (covenant executes autonomously)
✅ No conditional transfer by Asgaya (just bulletin board coordination)
✅ Information society service (MiCA/PSD2 exempt)
✅ No Kraken account needed (seller already owns BCH)


WHY THE PIVOT?
--------------

**RS052 Research (Core Regulatory Constraints):**

Key finding: The "uncrossable regulatory line" under MiCA Article 3(1):

> "Crypto-asset services" requiring CASP licensing include:
> - Custody of crypto-assets on behalf of clients
> - Exchange of crypto-assets for funds or other crypto-assets
> - **Conditional transfers executed by a central party**

**Old escrow model crossed all three lines:**
- Escrow held EUR (custody)
- Escrow bought BCH on Kraken (exchange)
- Escrow decided when to execute transfer (conditional transfer)

**Covenant model crosses ZERO lines:**
- No custody (seller posts own BCH)
- No exchange (seller already owns BCH, no purchase at settlement)
- No conditional transfer (covenant executes autonomously based on cryptographic signatures)

**Result:** Information society service exemption applies. No CASP licensing needed.

**See:** `docs/concepts/core-regulatory-constraints.md` for full RS052 analysis.


WHAT STAYED THE SAME
---------------------

Core principles preserved:
✅ Pull system (recipient controls timing)
✅ Volatility protection (30-second window, not hours)
✅ <1% total fees (still cheaper than legacy)
✅ No KYC (peer-to-peer coordination)
✅ Self-custody (users control BCH)
✅ Permissionless (anyone can participate)

**The covenant pivot preserved the pull system innovation while achieving regulatory
compliance. We didn't abandon the core vision - we found a better way to implement it.**

COVENANT SMART CONTRACT (CashScript)
-------------------------------------

The covenant is implemented in BCH CashScript (Bitcoin Script with helpful abstractions).

**Key functions:**

```
contract AsgayaRemittanceCovenant(
    pubkey merchantPubKey,
    pubkey recipientPubKey,
    pubkey sellerPubKey,
    pubkey senderRefundPubKey,
    int merchantAmount,      // €99.50 worth of BCH (calculated at maturity)
    int sellerAmount,        // €7.50 worth of BCH (overcollateralization + fee)
    int timeout              // 24 hours from creation
) {
    // Normal execution: Both merchant and recipient sign
    function mature(sig merchantSig, sig recipientSig) {
        require(checkSig(merchantSig, merchantPubKey));
        require(checkSig(recipientSig, recipientPubKey));

        // Output 1: merchantAmount → merchant address
        // Output 2: sellerAmount → seller address
    }

    // Timeout refund: After 24h, split refund to sender and seller
    function refundTimeout() {
        require(tx.time >= timeout);

        // Output 1: merchantAmount → sender refund address
        // Output 2: sellerAmount → seller address
    }

    // Early cancellation: Seller can cancel if no Bizum received within 5 min
    function cancelNoBizum(sig sellerSig) {
        require(checkSig(sellerSig, sellerPubKey));
        require(tx.time <= timeout - 23h 55min);  // Only within first 5 min

        // Output 1: ALL BCH → seller address (full refund)
    }
}
```

**EUR-denominated promise:**
The `merchantAmount` and `sellerAmount` are calculated at covenant maturity time using
real-time BCH/EUR spot price (e.g., Kraken API, CoinGecko). The covenant doesn't store
EUR values - it stores BCH amounts calculated to equal the EUR promise at maturity.


BULLETIN BOARD ARCHITECTURE
----------------------------

Asgaya operates as a **bulletin board** (peer-to-peer coordination layer):

Components:
1. **PostgreSQL database** (bounties, merchant listings, transaction history)
2. **REST API** (POST /bounties, GET /bounties/{code}, POST /covenants/sign)
3. **Android app** (merchant UI, recipient UI)
4. **BCH seller bot** (monitors bulletin board, accepts bounties automatically)
5. **Notification service** (SMS via Twilio for bounty created/funded/ready)

**Key principle:** Asgaya doesn't hold funds, doesn't execute transfers, doesn't judge
disputes. It's a **coordination service** (like GitHub for remittances).


EXCHANGE RATE SOURCES
----------------------

Asgaya uses multiple public APIs for transparent, verifiable exchange rates:

**BCH/EUR rate:**
- Primary: CoinGecko API (BCH/EUR spot price)
- Fallback: Kraken API (BCH/EUR trading pair)
- Cache: 1 minute (refresh at covenant creation for precision)

**EUR/VES rate (Venezuela blue dollar):**
- Primary: DolarAPI (Venezuelan parallel market rate)
- Cache: 5 minutes (updates frequently during trading hours)
- Example: 1 EUR ≈ 50,000 VES (official rate 36.5 VES is artificial, inaccessible)

**Display to users:**
- Sender (Spain): "€100 sends ~500,000 VES to Venezuela"
- Recipient (Venezuela): "Claim 500,000 VES at merchant (€100 equivalent)"
- Merchant: "Hand 500,000 VES cash, receive 0.0995 BCH (~€99.50)"

**All rates are publicly verifiable** (users can check CoinGecko, DolarAPI themselves).

CONCLUSION
==============================================

Asgaya represents a fundamental rethinking of crypto remittances:

**Previous attempts failed because:**
- Volatility made amounts unpredictable (recipient got less than expected)
- Custody triggered regulatory licensing (expensive, slow, risky)
- Push-based flows required instant claiming (timezone coordination problem)

**Asgaya succeeds by:**
- Covenant-based pull system (recipient controls timing, 30-second volatility window)
- No custody/intermediation (MiCA/PSD2 compliant, no licensing needed)
- Overcollateralization + EUR-denominated promise (merchant always gets full amount)
- BCH seller hedge mechanism (better than holding BCH in both price directions)
- <1% total fees (6.5× cheaper than Western Union/MoneyGram)
