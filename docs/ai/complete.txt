WHAT IS ASGAYA?
==============================================

Asgaya is a permissionless, peer-to-peer remittance protocol that enables cross-border
money transfers using Bitcoin Cash covenant smart contracts, with no custody, no
intermediation, and no KYC requirements.

**Tagline:** Bitcoin Cash remittances with <1% fees, no KYC, self-custody

**Key Innovation:** Covenant-based pull system eliminates volatility risk while
maintaining regulatory compliance (no CASP/PSD2 licensing needed under MiCA)


CORE REQUIREMENTS
------------------

Asgaya satisfies three fundamental requirements:

1. CHEAPER THAN LEGACY (<1% fees)
   - Traditional remittances: 6.49% average cost (World Bank)
   - Asgaya target: 1% total fee (sender pays)
   - On €100 transfer: Save €5.49 vs. Western Union/MoneyGram
   - Enabled by: No Kraken purchase, no custody overhead, peer-to-peer rails

2. PROMOTE BCH ADOPTION
   - Every transaction creates economic incentives for BCH accumulation
   - Merchants earn 0.5% per transaction in BCH
   - BCH sellers earn 0.5% + hedge benefits
   - Self-sustaining circular economy emerges naturally

3. PERMISSIONLESS
   - No KYC requirements (peer-to-peer coordination)
   - Minimal hardware (Android phone sufficient)
   - Minimal knowledge (if you can use WhatsApp, you can use Asgaya)
   - Self-custody (users control their BCH via covenant smart contracts)


FEE STRUCTURE (Updated May 11, 2026)

Total fee: 1% of transfer amount (sender pays)

Comparison: €1.00 (Asgaya) vs €6.49 (traditional) = **6.5× cheaper**

**Key change from v1.0:** No escrow operator, no Kraken exchange fee, no liquidity provider.

THE COVENANT MODEL (Updated May 10, 2026)
------------------------------------------

Asgaya uses **Bitcoin Cash CashScript covenants** (smart contracts) to enable trustless,
autonomous settlement without any party holding custody of funds.

**What is a covenant?**
A covenant is a Bitcoin Cash smart contract that:
- Holds BCH in a contract address (not controlled by any single party)
- Specifies conditions for fund distribution
- Executes autonomously when conditions are met (cryptographic signatures)
- Cannot be overridden by any human (immutable once created)

**Why covenants solve the regulatory problem:**
- No custody (BCH seller posts their own BCH, not held by Asgaya) ✅
- No intermediation (covenant executes autonomously, no entity controls distribution) ✅
- No conditional transfers by Asgaya (just bulletin board coordination) ✅
- Information society service (pure bulletin board = MiCA/PSD2 exempt) ✅

----------------------------

**Phase 1: Covenant Creation & Funding (5 minutes)**

1. **Sender (Iris in Spain) creates covenant:**

THE PULL SYSTEM
==============================================

KEY INNOVATION: Recipient Controls WHEN Execution Happens
----------------------------------------------------------

Traditional push-based remittances:
❌ Sender initiates → Funds move immediately → Recipient claims ASAP
❌ Volatility window = entire claim period (hours to days)
❌ Timezone coordination required (sender and recipient must be available)
❌ Recipient has no control over timing

Previous crypto attempts (2-step escrow):
❌ Sender pays escrow → Escrow holds EUR → Recipient claims → Escrow buys BCH
❌ Still requires recipient to claim within volatility window
❌ Escrow custody = CASP licensing required under MiCA

**Asgaya covenant pull system:**
✅ Sender creates covenant → Funds locked in smart contract → Recipient claims WHENEVER
✅ Volatility window = 30 seconds (only when recipient actually cashes out)
✅ No timezone coordination (recipient goes to merchant when convenient)
✅ Recipient has full control over execution timing


WHY THIS MATTERS (Venezuela Context)
-------------------------------------

Elena in Venezuela faces:
- Unreliable power (outages common)
- Unpredictable work schedule
- Distance to merchant (might be 30 min walk)
- Need to coordinate with merchant availability

**Pull system allows:**
- Elena can claim Monday morning (power reliable, she's free)

- **24-hour window gives flexibility without volatility risk**

**Covenant waits patiently. No pressure. No volatility exposure during wait.**


HOW VOLATILITY PROTECTION WORKS
--------------------------------

The covenant is **EUR-denominated** (futures contract):

**NOT:** "Merchant gets 0.0995 BCH" (fixed BCH amount)
**YES:** "Merchant gets €99.50 worth of BCH" (fixed EUR value, calculated at maturity)

Example:
- Covenant created: 1 BCH = €1,000 (estimated 0.1 BCH needed)
- BCH seller posts: 0.107 BCH (€107 at creation)
- Elena claims 12 hours later: 1 BCH = €950 (price dropped 5%)
- Merchant receives: €99.50 / €950 = **0.1047 BCH** (more BCH to reach €99.50 value)
- Seller receives: 0.107 - 0.1047 = **0.0023 BCH** (worth €2.19 at maturity)

**Merchant got full €99.50 value (protected from drop)**
**Seller absorbed the loss (that's what overcollateralization is for)**

THE SELLER HEDGE MECHANISM (Discovered May 11, 2026)
-----------------------------------------------------

**Key insight:** BCH sellers don't just earn a fee - they get a **built-in hedge** that
makes them BETTER OFF than just holding BCH in BOTH price directions.

**How the hedge works:**

Traditional BCH holder:
- Owns 0.107 BCH
- Fully exposed to price volatility
- If price drops 5% → Loses €5.35
- If price rises 5% → Gains €5.35

Covenant BCH seller:
- Posts 0.107 BCH to covenant
- Receives €100 fiat IMMEDIATELY (within 5 minutes, before price moves)
- Only ~0.007 BCH remains exposed to volatility (the surplus)
- **94% of value converted to stable fiat upfront**

**Example: BCH drops 5% (€1,000 → €950)**

Just holding 0.107 BCH:
- Value at maturity: 0.107 × €950 = €101.65
- Loss: €5.35

Covenant seller:
- €100 Bizum (received before drop, locked in at old price)
- 0.0023 BCH surplus (worth €2.19 at new price)
- Total: €100 + €2.19 = **€102.19**
- Better than holding by: €102.19 - €101.65 = **€0.54**

**Example: BCH rises 5% (€1,000 → €1,050)**

Just holding 0.107 BCH:
- Value at maturity: 0.107 × €1,050 = €112.35
- Gain: €5.35

Covenant seller:
- €100 Bizum (locked in, can't benefit from rise on this portion)
- 0.0122 BCH surplus (worth €12.81 at new price)
- Total: €100 + €12.81 = **€112.81**
- Better than holding by: €112.81 - €112.35 = **€0.46**

**RESULT: Seller wins in BOTH directions!** 🎯
- Price drops → Better by €0.54 (hedge protected fiat portion)
- Price rises → Better by €0.46 (surplus amplifies gains)
- Plus: Always earn €0.50 fixed fee regardless of price movement

==============================================
2.1 THE PULL SYSTEM — RECIPIENT-TRIGGERED EXECUTION
==============================================

**Concept Type:** Protocol Design Pattern
**Category:** Volatility Protection
**Innovation Level:** Core to Asgaya's differentiation

---

WHAT IT IS
----------

A remittance execution model where the **recipient triggers** BCH settlement when
they're ready to cash out, rather than the sender triggering immediate conversion.

**Traditional "push" model:**
```
Sender pays → Immediately convert to BCH → Send to recipient
├─ Time window: 20+ minutes (banking + blockchain delays)
├─ Volatility risk: 2%+ possible during transit
└─ Timezone coordination: Sender decides when, recipient must be ready
```

**Asgaya "pull" model:**
```
Sender creates covenant → BCH seller funds it → Recipient claims whenever ready
├─ Time window: 30 seconds (covenant execution time)
├─ Volatility risk: 0.1% typical (overcollateralization absorbs swings)
└─ No timezone coordination: Recipient controls timing completely
```

---

WHY IT EXISTS
-------------

**The volatility problem:** BCH price can move significantly in 20 minutes.

**Who gets hurt in push model:**
- Sender sends €100
- 20 minutes later (banking delays, processing time)
- BCH dropped 2%
- Recipient receives €98 worth
- **Recipient loses money through no fault of their own**

**The coordination problem:** Sender and recipient in different timezones.

**Who gets hurt in synchronized model:**
- Sender (Spain, 2pm) wants to send money
- Recipient (Venezuela, 8am) is asleep or busy
- Either sender waits, or recipient must wake up
- **Terrible UX, kills adoption**

**Remittances need:**
1. **Predictability** - Recipient knows exactly what they'll get
2. **Asynchronous** - No timezone coordination required
3. **Recipient control** - Cash out when ready, not when sender decides

**The pull system solves all three.**

---

THREE INNOVATIONS IN ONE
------------------------

### Innovation 1: Volatility Window Compression (20min → 30sec)

**Statistical reality:**
- BCH **CAN** move 2% in 20 minutes (happens regularly during volatile markets)
- BCH **CANNOT** move 2% in 30 seconds (except black swan events)

**Example:**
```
Traditional push model:
├─ Sender buys BCH: 10:00am
├─ BCH transfer confirms: 10:15am (blockchain delay)
├─ Recipient sells BCH: 10:20am
└─ Volatility window: 20 minutes → 2% risk

Asgaya pull model:
├─ Covenant funded: Day 1 (BCH locked, not moving)
├─ Recipient triggers: Day 2, 10:00am (whenever ready)
├─ Covenant executes: Day 2, 10:00:30am (30 seconds)
└─ Volatility window: 30 seconds → 0.1% risk
```

**Result:** Volatility risk effectively eliminated (99.5% reduction).

### Innovation 2: Timezone Independence (Required → Not Required)

**Old P2P bulletin board model (without covenants):**
```
10:00am Spain (4am Venezuela):
├─ Sender posts: "€100 available"
├─ Recipient must claim NOW (both online simultaneously)
├─ If recipient asleep → Miss opportunity
└─ Result: Timezone coordination kills UX
```

**Covenant-based pull system:**
```
Day 1, 10:00am Spain:
├─ Sender creates covenant (2 minutes)
├─ BCH seller funds covenant (5 minutes)
├─ Recipient notified: "24h window to claim"

Day 1 or Day 2, whenever:
├─ Recipient goes to merchant (their schedule)
├─ Covenant executes (30 seconds)
└─ Result: Send-and-forget restored, recipient empowered
```

**Result:** No coordination needed. Sender doesn't wait. Recipient not rushed.

### Innovation 3: Regulatory Compliance (Impossible → Viable)

**Old escrow model (why it failed):**
```
Central escrow holds EUR:
├─ Entity custody of client funds → CASP licensing required (MiCA Article 3)
├─ Entity provides conditional transfers → Payment intermediation (PSD2)
├─ Cost: €50K-200K legal fees, 12-18 months
└─ Result: Legally impossible for permissionless protocol
```

**Covenant-based model (why it works):**
```
Smart contract holds BCH:
├─ No entity custody (seller posts own BCH)
├─ No entity intermediation (covenant executes autonomously)
├─ No conditional transfers by Asgaya (just bulletin board)
└─ Result: Information society service = MiCA/PSD2 exempt ✅
```

**Key regulatory insight (RS052 research):**
> "The uncrossable line under MiCA Article 3(1)(a-e) is custody + intermediation +
> conditional transfers. Covenants cross ZERO of these lines."

**Result:** Legally viable, permissionless architecture.

HOW OVERCOLLATERALIZATION PROTECTS
-----------------------------------

**The problem:** What if BCH price drops while covenant is waiting?

**Example scenario:**
```
Day 1, 10:00am: Covenant created by Iris
Day 1, 10:05am: BCH seller locks 0.107 BCH (€107 at €1,000/BCH)
Day 1, 2:00pm:  BCH drops 5% (€1,000 → €950)
Day 2, 9:00am:  Elena claims at merchant

At claim time:
├─ 0.107 BCH now worth: €101.65 (down from €107)
├─ Merchant needs: €99.50 worth
└─ Remaining for seller: €2.15 (down from €7.50 surplus)
```

**Without overcollateralization:**
```
Seller posted: 0.1 BCH (€100 at creation)
After 5% drop: 0.1 BCH = €95
Merchant needs: €99.50 worth
Result: Shortfall! Deal would fail ❌
```

**With 7% overcollateralization:**
```
Seller posted: 0.107 BCH (€107 at creation)
After 5% drop: 0.107 BCH = €101.65
Merchant needs: €99.50 / €950 = 0.1047 BCH
Seller gets back: 0.107 - 0.1047 = 0.0023 BCH (€2.18)
Result: Deal succeeds! Merchant gets full €99.50 ✅
```

**Protection thresholds:**
```
BCH price change  │ Result
──────────────────┼────────────────────────────────
±0% to ±3%        │ Fully covered, seller keeps most surplus
±3% to ±5%        │ Fully covered, seller keeps small surplus  
±5% to ±7%        │ Fully covered, seller keeps tiny surplus
>±7%              │ MARGIN CALL - seller must add BCH or refund
```

**Statistical reality (based on BCH historical volatility):**
- BCH moves ±3% in 24h: **Common** (happens ~30% of days)
- BCH moves ±5% in 24h: **Occasional** (happens ~10% of days)
- BCH moves ±7% in 24h: **Rare** (happens ~5% of days)
- BCH moves >10% in 24h: **Very rare** (black swan events)

**Result:** 7% buffer covers 95%+ of normal daily volatility.

---

MARGIN CALL MECHANISM
----------------------

**If BCH price drops >7% during the 24h window:**

1. **Alert sent to seller (automated monitoring):**
   ```
   ⚠️ MARGIN CALL - Bounty #4729
   
   BCH dropped 8% (€1,000 → €920)
   Current collateral: 0.107 BCH (€98.44)
   Required: €99.50 worth (0.1081 BCH at €920)
   
   Shortfall: 0.0011 BCH (€1.01)
   
   Actions:
   [Add 0.0015 BCH] (recommended buffer)
   [Let timeout refund] (no loss, just time wasted)
   
   You have 60 minutes to decide.
   ```

2. **Seller decides:**
   - **Add BCH:** Top up covenant to maintain buffer (bet on recovery)
   - **Let refund:** Timeout expires, split refund occurs (avoid more exposure)

3. **If seller adds BCH:**
   - Covenant continues normally
   - If Elena claims later, merchant gets full €99.50
   - Seller gets back original + topped-up amount (minus merchant portion)

4. **If seller doesn't respond within 60 min:**
   - Covenant triggers early refund (doesn't wait full 24h)
   - Split refund: Merchant portion → Iris, Seller portion → Seller
   - Iris notified: "Deal cancelled due to margin call, €99.50 refunded (in BCH)"

**Why 60-minute grace period:**
- Sellers need time to respond (might be asleep, traveling)
- But can't wait too long (price might drop further)
- 60 min is balance between responsiveness and fairness

**Seller's risk mitigation:**
- Monitor BCH price (automated bot alerts)
- Maintain BCH buffer beyond minimum (post 10% not 7% if cautious)
- Only accept bounties during stable market periods
- Use stop-loss strategies (auto-cancel if volatility spikes)

---

TRADE-OFFS
----------

**Adds complexity (vs. simple push model):**
- Four-party coordination (sender, BCH seller, merchant, recipient)
- Covenant contract deployment (CashScript technical knowledge)
- Overcollateralization capital requirements (BCH sellers need inventory)
- Margin call monitoring (if BCH drops >7%)
- More moving parts than "sender pays, immediately convert, send"

**But eliminates complexity (vs. synchronized P2P model):**
- ✅ No timezone coordination needed (sender and recipient asynchronous)
- ✅ No "both online at same time" requirement
- ✅ No sender waiting for recipient to wake up / be ready
- ✅ No recipient rushing because sender wants to pay NOW

**And provides benefits:**
- ✅ 99.9%+ accuracy in EUR → VES conversion (overcollateralization absorbs volatility)
- ✅ Recipient sees exact VES amount before claiming (predictable)
- ✅ Send-and-forget UX restored (sender doesn't wait for execution)
- ✅ Recipient-controlled timing (claim when convenient, not when sender decides)
- ✅ MiCA/PSD2 compliant (no custody, no intermediation)
- ✅ Permissionless (anyone can be BCH seller, anyone can create covenant)

**Net verdict:** Complexity worth it for volatility protection + async UX + regulatory compliance.

---

WHY "PULL" NOT "PUSH" (ANALOGY)
--------------------------------

**Restaurant gift card vs. food delivery**

**Push model** = Sender orders food delivery for recipient
- Food made immediately when sender orders
- Delivered whenever it arrives (sender's timing)
- If recipient not home, delivery fails or food gets cold
- Recipient has no control over when food arrives
- **Sender decides timing, recipient must adapt**

**Pull model** = Sender buys gift card for recipient
- Gift card sits safely (no expiration, no spoilage)
- Recipient redeems whenever hungry (their schedule)
- Restaurant makes food fresh when redeemed (not hours earlier)
- Recipient has full control over timing
- **Recipient decides timing, sender just enables**

**Asgaya's covenant = gift card that guarantees freshness:**
- Sender creates covenant (gift card purchased)
- BCH locked safely (gift card sits in wallet)
- Recipient claims whenever ready (redeems gift card)
- Covenant executes instantly (restaurant makes fresh food in 30 seconds)
- **Pull system: Recipient empowerment + freshness guarantee**

2.2 OVERCOLLATERALIZED BOUNTY CONTRACTS
==============================================

**Status:** Core Innovation (Discovered May 10, 2026)
**Priority:** Enables compliant pull system
**Category:** Smart Contract Architecture

---

THE PROBLEM WE SOLVED
----------------------

Asgaya's original **pull system** elegantly solved cryptocurrency volatility:
- Sender's EUR sat safely in escrow (zero volatility)
- Escrow only bought BCH when recipient was at merchant and ready
- Volatility window compressed to ~30 seconds
- Sender could "send and forget" — transaction completed on recipient's schedule

**But this required a central escrow** that:
- Held client EUR (custody)
- Provided conditional transfer services (intermediation)
- **→ Triggered MiCA CASP licensing requirement**
- **→ Made the architecture legally impossible to operate**

On May 9, 2026, we documented why central escrow fails regulatory compliance.

**On May 10, 2026, we figured out how to get the pull system back.**

---

THE BREAKTHROUGH
----------------

> **What if the seller posts the collateral, and the smart contract is the escrow?**

Instead of a **central entity** holding funds, a **CashScript covenant** (BCH smart contract) 
holds **overcollateralized BCH posted by the seller**.

The contract only releases funds when both conditions are met:
1. **Seller confirmed:** "I received Bizum from sender" (automated bot signature)
2. **Merchant confirmed:** "I gave cash to recipient" (cryptographic signature)

**If BCH price moves while waiting, the overcollateralization absorbs the volatility.**

Excess BCH returns to the seller. The recipient gets exactly the promised amount of local currency.

**Key insight:** The covenant is **autonomous code**, not a service provider. 
No entity custody. No entity intermediation. Just code enforcing conditions.

---

CASHSCRIPT COVENANT SPECIFICATION
----------------------------------

**CashScript** is a high-level language for Bitcoin Cash contracts (compiles to Script).

**Asgaya Remittance Covenant structure:**

```cashscript
contract AsgayaRemittanceCovenant(
    // Parties
    pubkey merchantPubKey,
    pubkey recipientPubKey,
    pubkey sellerPubKey,
    pubkey senderRefundPubKey,
    
    // Amounts (in satoshis, calculated at covenant creation)
    int merchantAmount,      // €99.50 worth of BCH (at maturity rate)
    int sellerAmount,        // €7.50 worth of BCH (overcollateralization + fee)
    
    // Timing
    int timeout              // 24 hours from creation (Unix timestamp)
) {
    // Function 1: Normal execution (both parties sign)
    function mature(sig merchantSig, sig recipientSig) {
        // Verify both signatures
        require(checkSig(merchantSig, merchantPubKey));
        require(checkSig(recipientSig, recipientPubKey));
        
        // Distribute funds:
        // Output 1: merchantAmount → merchant address
        // Output 2: sellerAmount → seller address
    }
    
    // Function 2: Timeout refund (after 24h, split refund)
    function refundTimeout() {
        // Check timeout expired
        require(tx.time >= timeout);
        
        // Split refund:
        // Output 1: merchantAmount → sender refund address (Iris gets BCH back)
        // Output 2: sellerAmount → seller address (Seller keeps fee + buffer)
    }
    
    // Function 3: Early cancellation (seller cancels if no Bizum within 5 min)
    function cancelNoBizum(sig sellerSig) {
        require(checkSig(sellerSig, sellerPubKey));
        require(tx.time <= timeout - 23h55min);  // Only within first 5 min
        
        // Full refund to seller:
        // Output: ALL BCH → seller address
    }
}
```

**Key properties:**
- **Autonomous execution:** No entity can override the contract
- **Non-discretionary:** Code enforces conditions, not human judgment
- **Trustless:** All parties can verify contract logic before engaging
- **On-chain:** Deployed to BCH blockchain, publicly verifiable

---

EUR-DENOMINATED PROMISE (HOW IT WORKS)
---------------------------------------

The covenant is **EUR-denominated** (promises EUR value, not fixed BCH amount).

**At covenant creation:**
```
Iris creates covenant: "Elena will receive €100 worth of BCH"
Current BCH price: €1,000 per BCH
Estimated BCH needed: 0.1 BCH
Seller posts: 0.107 BCH (7% overcollateralization)
```

**At covenant maturity (12 hours later):**
```
Current BCH price: €1,020 per BCH (price rose 2%)
Merchant receives: €99.50 / €1,020 = 0.09755 BCH
Seller receives: 0.107 - 0.09755 = 0.00945 BCH (€9.64)

Merchant got: €99.50 worth ✅ (as promised)
Seller got: €7 overcollateralization + €0.50 fee + €2.14 price gain = €9.64 total
```

**If price had dropped 2%:**
```
Current BCH price: €980 per BCH
Merchant receives: €99.50 / €980 = 0.10153 BCH  
Seller receives: 0.107 - 0.10153 = 0.00547 BCH (€5.36)

Merchant got: €99.50 worth ✅ (still as promised)
Seller got: €7 - €2 price drop + €0.50 fee = €5.50 worth of BCH
```

**Key:** Merchant always gets full EUR-equivalent. Price risk on seller's surplus.

RATE CALCULATION AT MATURITY

**How covenant knows EUR/BCH rate at execution time:**

1. **Oracle-free approach (Phase 0-1):**

2. **Future oracle approach (Phase 2+):**

   - Fully autonomous (no off-chain app involvement)
   - More trustless, but adds complexity

**Phase 0-1 is sufficient:** Rate calculated by app, amounts hardcoded in transaction.
Covenant just distributes what transaction specifies.

FEE DISTRIBUTION MECHANICS
--------------------------

**Total fee: 1% of transaction value**

On €100 transaction:
- Total fee pool: €1.00
- BCH seller: €0.50 (0.5%)
- Merchant: €0.50 (0.5%)

**At covenant maturity:**
```
Transaction: €100 (what Iris intended to send)
Fee: €1.00 (what Iris paid)
Total locked in covenant: €101 worth of BCH (€100 + €1 fee)

Minus overcollateralization adjustment:
Actually locked: €107 worth (need buffer for volatility)

Distribution:
├─ Merchant: €99.50 worth (€100 - €0.50 merchant fee)
├─ Seller fee: €0.50 worth
└─ Seller buffer: €7.00 worth
Total to seller: €7.50 worth
```

**Why merchant gets €99.50 not €100:**
- Merchant earns €0.50 fee for providing cash + location
- So they hand €99 worth of VES to Elena
- And receive €99.50 worth of BCH
- Net: €0.50 profit

**Elena receives:** €99 worth of VES (after both fees deducted)

2.3 CORE REGULATORY CONSTRAINTS (RS052)
==============================================

**Research:** RS052 - Compliance Architecture
**Date:** May 2026
**Impact:** Drove May 10 covenant pivot

---

THE UNCROSSABLE LINE
--------------------

**MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation)** came into force December 30, 2024.

**Article 3(1) defines "crypto-asset services" requiring CASP (Crypto-Asset Service 
Provider) licensing:**

```
(a) The custody and administration of crypto-assets on behalf of third parties;
(b) The operation of a trading platform for crypto-assets;
(c) The exchange of crypto-assets for funds or other crypto-assets;
(d) The execution of orders for crypto-assets on behalf of third parties;
(e) Providing transfer services for crypto-assets on behalf of third parties.
```

**If Asgaya triggers ANY of these, we need:**
- €50K-200K in legal/compliance costs
- 12-18 months licensing process
- Ongoing compliance audits
- Impossible for permissionless protocol

---

WHY ESCROW MODEL FAILED
------------------------

**Old architecture (pre-May 10):**
```
1. Sender pays Asgaya escrow (EUR held by entity)
2. Escrow holds EUR until recipient ready
3. Recipient claims at merchant
4. Escrow buys BCH on Kraken
5. Escrow sends BCH to merchant
```

**MiCA violations:**
- ❌ **(a) Custody:** Asgaya entity holds EUR "on behalf of third parties" (sender's money)
- ❌ **(c) Exchange:** Asgaya entity "exchanges crypto-assets for funds" (buys BCH with EUR)
- ❌ **(e) Transfer service:** Asgaya entity "provides transfer services" (conditional EUR→BCH)

**Result:** Requires CASP licensing. **Legally impossible for permissionless protocol.**

---

WHY COVENANT MODEL WORKS
-------------------------

**New architecture (May 10+):**
```
1. Sender creates covenant (smart contract)
2. BCH seller posts their own BCH (not holding client funds)
3. Sender pays BCH seller directly via Bizum (peer-to-peer)
4. Covenant executes autonomously when conditions met
5. Covenant distributes BCH (code, not entity)
```

**MiCA compliance:**
- ✅ **(a) Custody:** No entity custody (seller posts own BCH, covenant is code)
- ✅ **(c) Exchange:** No entity exchange (seller already owns BCH, no purchase at settlement)
- ✅ **(e) Transfer:** No entity transfer service (covenant executes autonomously)

**Key principle:**
> "Asgaya provides information coordination (bulletin board), not financial services.
> Smart contract executes autonomously. No entity discretion."

**Result:** **Information society service** exemption applies (MiCA doesn't regulate pure tech platforms).

---

PSD2 (PAYMENT SERVICES DIRECTIVE)
----------------------------------

**PSD2 defines "payment services" requiring licensing:**

```
Annex I: Payment services subject to this Directive

(3) Execution of payment transactions:
    (a) execution of direct debits
    (b) execution of payment transactions through a payment card
    (c) execution of credit transfers
    (d) execution of payment transactions where funds are covered by a credit line

(7) Issuing and acquiring of payment instruments

(8) Money remittance
```

**Old escrow model violated PSD2:**
- ❌ **(8) Money remittance:** Asgaya escrow "transfers funds" from sender to recipient
- ❌ Required Payment Institution (PI) licensing

**New covenant model complies:**
- ✅ **No remittance service:** Sender pays BCH seller (Bizum P2P, already regulated)
- ✅ **Covenant is tech:** Smart contract executes, not entity providing payment service
- ✅ **Merchant sells VES:** Local P2P cash trade (not regulated remittance)

**Result:** No PSD2 Payment Institution license needed.

---

THE INFORMATION SOCIETY SERVICE EXEMPTION
------------------------------------------

**EU E-Commerce Directive (2000/31/EC)** defines "information society service":

```
Article 2(a): Information society service:
"Any service normally provided for remuneration, at a distance, by electronic means,
and at the individual request of a recipient of services."

Examples: Search engines, classified ads, bulletin boards, coordination platforms.
```

**Asgaya as information society service:**
- Provides bulletin board (list of available bounties)
- Coordinates parties (sender, seller, merchant, recipient)
- Hosts app for covenant creation (tool, not service)
- Does NOT hold funds, execute payments, or intermediate transfers

**Protected by E-Commerce Directive:**
- Articles 12-15: "Mere conduit" exemption
- Platform not liable for user-generated content (bounties)
- Platform doesn't need financial services licensing

**Like:** eBay (connects buyers/sellers, doesn't sell), Craigslist (classified ads), 
GitHub (hosts code repos).

**Unlike:** PayPal (holds funds, executes payments), Wise (money remittance), 
Coinbase (exchanges crypto, holds custody).

---

THE THREE SAFE DESIGN PRINCIPLES
---------------------------------

**To stay in information society service exemption:**

### 1. NO CUSTODY

❌ **Don't:** Hold client funds (EUR, BCH, any asset)
✅ **Do:** Let parties control their own funds
✅ **Asgaya:** BCH seller posts own BCH, sender pays seller directly

### 2. NO INTERMEDIATION

❌ **Don't:** Provide conditional transfers (entity decides when to execute)
✅ **Do:** Let autonomous code execute based on cryptographic proofs
✅ **Asgaya:** Covenant executes when both signatures present (no entity discretion)

### 3. NO PROTOCOL FEE

❌ **Don't:** Take protocol cut of transactions (signals commercial service)
✅ **Do:** Zero protocol revenue (pure coordination)
✅ **Asgaya:** Seller earns 0.5%, Merchant earns 0.5%, Protocol earns 0%

**If Asgaya crosses ANY of these lines → Becomes regulated financial service.**

TRADE-OFFS ACCEPTED
-------------------

**To achieve regulatory compliance, we accept:**

1. **Seller capital requirements:**
   - BCH sellers need inventory (can't just buy on demand)
   - Limits who can be a seller (not everyone has 10+ BCH)
   - But: Miners are natural sellers (already have BCH)

2. **Overcollateralization capital inefficiency:**
   - 7% extra BCH locked per transaction
   - Seller capital tied up (not earning yield elsewhere)
   - But: Capital cycles every 2-6 hours (acceptable lockup)

3. **Margin call complexity:**
   - Sellers must monitor BCH price
   - Must respond within 60 min if >7% drop
   - But: Automated bots handle this (not manual)

4. **No protocol revenue:**
   - Asgaya Foundation gets zero income from transactions
   - Must rely on grants, donations, or other funding
   - But: Keeps protocol permissionless and compliant

**Net:** These trade-offs are acceptable to achieve legal viability.

   - Question: "How to structure Asgaya Foundation for maximum protection?"
   - Deliverable: Entity structure recommendation

**Cost estimate:** €15K-25K for all three opinions (one-time expense, critical protection).

**ROI:** Priceless (prevents €50K-200K CASP licensing + potential fines).

==============================================
3.1 FEE SPLITTING MODEL
==============================================

**Decision Date:** May 11, 2026 (Updated for covenant architecture)
**Status:** Final for Phase 0
**Authority:** Authoritative fee structure

---

THE DECISION
------------

**Two-way fee split (no protocol fee):**

On a €100 transfer with 1% total fee:
- **BCH Seller:** €0.50 (0.5%) - Posts overcollateralized BCH, earns for capital + risk
- **Merchant:** €0.50 (0.5%) - Hands cash, provides location + local presence  
- **Protocol (Asgaya):** €0.00 (0%) - No protocol cut = pure bulletin board

**Recipient receives:** €99 worth of VES (after both fees deducted)

---

RATIONALE
---------

### Why 2-Way Split (Not 3-Way)

**Old escrow model had 3-way split:**
- Escrow operator: 0.247%
- Merchant: 0.247%
- Liquidity Provider: 0.247%
- Kraken fee: 0.26% (deducted first)

**New covenant model simplified to 2-way:**
- BCH seller (replaced escrow + LP roles): 0.5%
- Merchant: 0.5%
- No Kraken (seller already owns BCH)
- No protocol fee (regulatory compliance)

**Why this works:**
- BCH seller performs both old roles (capital provider + covenant signer)
- Consolidating roles = simpler, fewer parties
- 0.5% each is enough to attract participants (validated in Phase 0)

### Why No Protocol Fee

**Regulatory reason (primary):**
> "Taking a protocol cut signals Asgaya provides a commercial service, triggering
> MiCA/PSD2 licensing. Zero protocol fee = information society service exemption."

**If protocol took 0.2% fee:**
- Regulators: "You're earning revenue from transfers → You're a payment service"
- Asgaya: "We just coordinate" 
- Regulators: "But you profit from it → You're providing the service"
- Result: CASP/PI licensing required ❌

**With zero protocol fee:**
- Regulators: "What's your revenue model?"
- Asgaya: "Zero. We're a non-profit coordination platform."
- Regulators: "Then you're information society service → Exempt"
- Result: No licensing needed ✅

**Trade-off accepted:** Asgaya Foundation needs grants/donations, can't self-fund from fees.

### Why 0.5% Each (Not More/Less)

**For BCH sellers:**
- 0.5% on €100 = €0.50 per transaction
- Plus hedge benefit (~10% extra, so ~€0.55 effective)
- 100 transactions/day = €55/day = €1,650/month
- On €10k capital = 16.5% monthly ROI = attractive ✅

**If 0.25%:**
- €0.25 per transaction not worth automation effort
- Sellers wouldn't participate
- Network fails ❌

**If 1%:**
- €1 per transaction = very attractive for sellers
- But then merchant only gets 0% (seller + merchant = 1% total)
- Merchants wouldn't participate
- Network fails ❌

**0.5% each is balance:** Both parties sufficiently motivated.

**For merchants:**
- 0.5% on €100 = €0.50 per transaction
- 20 transactions/day = €10/day = €300/month extra income
- For small Venezuelan bodega, this is significant (30%+ of daily profit)
- Worthwhile to learn app + onboard ✅

**Phase 0 will validate:** Is 0.5% actually attractive? Or need adjustment to 0.4%/0.6% split?

---

COMPARISON TO LEGACY FEES
--------------------------

**Asgaya: 1% total**
- Sender pays: €100
- Fees: €1 (BCH seller €0.50, Merchant €0.50)
- Recipient gets: €99 equivalent

**Western Union: 6-8% total**
- Sender pays: €100
- Fees: €6-8 (company profit + agent commissions)
- Recipient gets: €92-94 equivalent

**MoneyGram: 5-7% total**

**Wise: 3-4% total** (best traditional service)

**Asgaya saves sender: €5-7 per €100 = 5-7× cheaper**

---

BCH BUYER SECONDARY MARKET (Optional)
--------------------------------------

**After merchant receives BCH, they can optionally sell it for fiat:**

**BCH buyer bulletin board** (Phase 1+ feature):
```
Merchant posts: "Sell 0.0995 BCH for VES"
BCH buyers compete with offers:
├─ Buyer A: 500,000 VES (0.2% spread)
├─ Buyer B: 499,000 VES (0.4% spread)  
└─ Buyer C: 498,500 VES (0.5% spread)

Merchant selects best offer.
Same covenant mechanism (merchant becomes sender, BCH buyer becomes recipient).
```

**Educational framing (in docs):**
- We show 0.5% spread in examples (same as reward)
- Makes clear: Selling immediately = lose full reward to spread
- Incentivizes holding BCH (Asgaya's adoption mission)

**In reality:**
- BCH buyers might compete with 0.2-0.3% spreads (better for merchant)
- But we don't promote this in docs (focus on BCH holding benefits)

**This creates circular economy:**
```
Iris (Spain) → Elena (Venezuela) via merchant
Merchant accumulates BCH
Merchant → BCH buyer via new merchant (same covenant flow)
BCH buyer accumulates BCH or trades further
Circular flow continues...
```

---

ALTERNATIVE MODELS REJECTED
----------------------------

### Alternative 1: Equal Three-Way Split

**Proposal:** Seller 0.33%, Merchant 0.33%, Protocol 0.33%

**Rejected because:**
- Protocol fee triggers regulatory scrutiny (MiCA/PSD2 risk)
- 0.33% each too small to attract participants
- Needs 150 transactions/day to reach €50/day income
- Phase 0 unlikely to achieve that volume

### Alternative 2: Dynamic Fees Based on Volume

**Proposal:** Higher % for small transactions, lower % for large

Example:
- €50 transfer: 2% fee (€1)
- €100 transfer: 1% fee (€1)
- €500 transfer: 0.5% fee (€2.50)

**Rejected because:**
- Adds complexity (users prefer simple, predictable fees)
- Corridor-dependent tuning needed (complex)
- Phase 0 can't validate multiple fee tiers
- Better to keep simple 1% flat fee, adjust later if needed

### Alternative 3: Sender Chooses Fee (Market-Based)

**Proposal:** Sender offers fee, sellers compete

Example:
- Iris offers 0.8% fee
- Sellers bid: "I'll do it for 0.6%" / "I'll do it for 0.5%"
- Iris picks lowest bid

**Rejected because:**
- Adds negotiation friction (slows transaction)
- Race to bottom (fees too low to be sustainable)
- Merchants not involved in negotiation (confusing UX)
- Phase 0 needs simplicity, not complexity

**May reconsider in Phase 2+** if market conditions change.

---

FUTURE FEE ADJUSTMENTS
----------------------

**Fee split is NOT hardcoded in protocol.** It's a parameter that can be adjusted.

**Triggers for adjustment:**

1. **Seller shortage:** If <5 active sellers in corridor
   - Consider: Increase seller fee to 0.6%, reduce merchant to 0.4%
   - Or: Accept that corridor isn't viable yet

2. **Merchant shortage:** If <10 active merchants in region
   - Consider: Increase merchant fee to 0.6%, reduce seller to 0.4%
   - Or: Recruit more merchants via direct outreach

3. **Excessive profits:** If sellers/merchants earning >>expected ROI
   - Consider: Reduce total fee to 0.8% (0.4% each)
   - Pass savings to recipients (competitive advantage)

4. **Corridor-specific tuning:** If Spain→Venezuela needs different split than US→Mexico
   - Consider: Corridor-specific fee tables
   - Adds complexity, only if necessary

**Phase 0 goal:** Validate 1% / 0.5%-0.5% split works as designed.

==============================================
3.2 TWO-STEP SETTLEMENT TIMING
==============================================

**Decision Date:** Original concept (2024), Updated May 10, 2026 (covenant pivot)
**Status:** Core to Asgaya's differentiation
**Category:** Volatility Protection

---

THE DECISION
------------

**Use two-step settlement with covenant-based pull execution:**

1. **Step 1: Covenant creation & funding (Asynchronous)**
   - Sender creates covenant (EUR-denominated promise)
   - BCH seller posts overcollateralized BCH
   - Sender pays seller via Bizum (fiat-to-fiat)
   - Covenant waits (BCH locked safely, not moving)

2. **Step 2: Recipient-triggered execution (When ready)**
   - Recipient goes to merchant (their schedule, 0-24h window)
   - Both parties co-sign covenant
   - Covenant executes instantly (30-second settlement)
   - Merchant receives BCH, seller receives surplus

**Key: Settlement happens in TWO distinct steps with a pause between them.**

---

HOW THIS DECISION MEETS ENGINEERING REQUIREMENTS
-------------------------------------------------

**Asgaya's engineering requirements (from core architecture):**

1. **Permissionless** - Anyone can participate
2. **Minimal hardware** - Android phone sufficient  
3. **Minimal knowledge** - WhatsApp-level complexity
4. **Fail-safe** - Power outages, unreliable internet (Venezuela context)
5. **Eliminate volatility** - Predictable EUR → VES conversion
6. **Self-custody** - Users control BCH
7. **Regulatory compliance** - No CASP/PSD2 licensing (added May 10)

**How two-step settlement achieves these:**

### Requirement 1: Permissionless ✅

**One-step (instant settlement):**
```
Sender → Instant BCH purchase → Immediate send → Recipient must claim now
Requires: Sender have BCH (or access to exchange)
Result: Not permissionless (need exchange account, KYC)
```

**Two-step (covenant pull):**
```
Sender → Create covenant → Pay seller Bizum → Done (send-and-forget)
Requires: Only Bizum app (everyone in Spain already has)
Result: Permissionless (no exchange, no BCH knowledge needed)
```

**Two-step enables permissionless sender experience.**

### Requirement 2: Minimal Hardware ✅

**One-step:**
```
Recipient needs: BCH wallet, private key management, exchange access to sell BCH
Hardware: Smartphone with crypto wallet app
Knowledge: Understand addresses, fees, confirmations, exchanges
```

**Two-step:**
```
Recipient needs: Show code, receive cash, tap confirm
Hardware: Smartphone with Asgaya app (or even just SMS notification)
Knowledge: "Go to merchant, show code, get cash"
```

**Two-step enables minimal-knowledge recipient experience.**

### Requirement 3: Fail-Safe (Venezuela Power Outages) ✅

**One-step synchronized:**
```
Sender and recipient must both be online simultaneously.
If recipient's power is out → Transaction fails
If internet down → Can't claim
Result: Unreliable in Venezuelan infrastructure
```

**Two-step asynchronous:**
```
Sender creates covenant when they have power/internet.
Covenant waits on blockchain (doesn't need recipient online).
Recipient claims later when power/internet available.
Result: Works despite unreliable infrastructure ✅
```

**Two-step is infrastructure-resilient.**

### Requirement 4: Eliminate Volatility ✅

**One-step:**
```
Sender buys BCH: 10:00am (€100 = 0.1 BCH)
BCH in transit: 10:00am - 10:20am (20 min window)
Recipient sells BCH: 10:20am (0.1 BCH = €98 if price dropped 2%)
Volatility window: 20 minutes = 2%+ risk
```

**Two-step:**
```
Covenant created: Day 1 (BCH locked, not moving)
Recipient triggers: Day 2 (whenever ready)
Covenant executes: 30 seconds (only movement window)
Volatility window: 30 seconds = 0.1% typical risk
```

**Two-step compresses volatility window from 20 minutes to 30 seconds.**

### Requirement 5: Self-Custody ✅

**One-step with central service:**
```
Service holds funds during transit (custody)
Service decides when to execute (intermediation)
Result: Not self-custody, triggers licensing
```

**Two-step with covenant:**
```
Smart contract holds BCH (autonomous code, not entity)
BCH seller posts own BCH (not holding client funds)
Parties control execution (co-signing)
Result: Self-custody maintained ✅
```

**Two-step preserves self-custody while coordinating parties.**

### Requirement 6: Regulatory Compliance ✅

**One-step escrow:**
```
Escrow holds EUR (custody) → CASP licensing
Escrow buys BCH (exchange) → CASP licensing
Escrow decides execution (intermediation) → PI licensing
Result: €50K-200K compliance costs, legally impossible
```

**Two-step covenant:**
```
No custody (seller posts own BCH)
No intermediation (covenant executes autonomously)
No conditional transfer by entity (just bulletin board)
Result: Information society service, no licensing ✅
```

**Two-step achieves regulatory compliance.**

---

TRADE-OFFS
----------

**Adds complexity:**
- Four-party coordination (vs. two-party direct send)
- Covenant deployment (vs. simple P2P transaction)
- Overcollateralization (7% capital tied up)
- Timeout handling (24h window management)

**But provides benefits:**
- ✅ 99.5% volatility reduction (20 min → 30 sec window)
- ✅ Asynchronous UX (no timezone coordination)
- ✅ Recipient control (claim when ready, not when sender decides)
- ✅ Regulatory compliance (no licensing needed)
- ✅ Permissionless (anyone can create covenant)
- ✅ Infrastructure-resilient (works despite power outages)

**Net: Complexity is worth it for volatility protection + async UX + compliance.**

---

ALTERNATIVES CONSIDERED
-----------------------

### Alternative 1: One-Step Instant Settlement

**Proposal:** Sender buys BCH → Sends immediately → Recipient sells immediately

**Why rejected:**
- Volatility window too long (20+ minutes)
- Sender and recipient must coordinate timing (timezone problem)
- Requires recipient have exchange access (not permissionless)
- Previous crypto remittances failed for exactly this reason

### Alternative 2: Stablecoin Bridge

**Proposal:** Use USDT/USDC on BCH (SLP tokens) instead of BCH

**Why rejected:**
- Stablecoins are "e-money" under MiCA → EMI licensing required
- Tether/Circle don't support BCH SLP (only Ethereum/Tron)
- Adds centralized issuer dependency (Tether can freeze)
- BCH community prefers native BCH, not wrapped tokens

### Alternative 3: Atomic Swaps (Fully Trustless P2P)

**Proposal:** Atomic swaps between EUR/BCH without covenant

**Why rejected:**
- No atomic swap support for fiat (EUR can't be locked in smart contract)
- Would need EUR stablecoin (see Alternative 2 issues)
- Adds technical complexity (HTLC contracts, hash preimage reveals)
- Overkill for this use case (covenant is simpler)

---

FUTURE EVOLUTION
----------------

**Phase 0-1: Two-step with manual app interaction**
- Sender creates covenant via app
- Recipient claims via app
- Merchant confirms via app
- All steps require smartphone interaction

**Phase 2: Two-step with reduced interaction**
- Sender creates covenant via app (still manual)
- Recipient uses RFID card (tap merchant's phone, no app needed)

**The two-step model stays, but friction reduces over time.**

==============================================
3.3 DISPUTE RESOLUTION
==============================================

**Decision Date:** May 11, 2026 (Simplified for covenant architecture)
**Status:** Phase 0 approach (no formal system)
**Category:** Risk Management

---

THE DECISION
------------

**Phase 0: No formal dispute resolution system**

- Covenant handles everything autonomously
- No mediator, no investigation, no arbitration
- Trusted parties only (sender's family/friends)

**Dispute outcomes:**
```
Both sign within 24h → Covenant executes automatically
Timeout (no signatures) → Covenant refunds automatically (split refund)
One party refuses → Timeout refund after 24h
```

**No human judgment. No entity discretion. Pure autonomous execution.**

---

RATIONALE
---------

### Why No Mediator

**If mediator can override covenant:**
- Mediator becomes "payment intermediation service" (PSD2 violation)
- Mediator "administers crypto-assets on behalf of parties" (MiCA Article 3)
- Result: Requires PI/CASP licensing ❌

**If mediator just investigates but can't override:**
- What's the point? Covenant executes regardless of investigation
- Adds complexity without changing outcome
- Better to skip mediator entirely

**Covenant is autonomous or it's not.** We chose fully autonomous.

### Why Trusted Parties in Phase 0

**Phase 0 scope:**
- 50-100 transactions
- Sender's family/friends only
- Single corridor: Spain → Venezuela
- Personal relationships (Iris trusts Elena, Elena trusts local merchant)

**Expected disputes in Phase 0:** **ZERO**

- Sender won't scam own family
- Family member recipient won't steal from merchant (family vouched for them)
- Merchant won't scam recipient (merchant is sender's family/friend in Venezuela)

**If trusted parties dispute:** Something seriously wrong, halt Phase 0, investigate manually.

**Phase 0 is NOT public network.** It's closed trial with trusted participants.

### V1: Social Media Transparency (Future)

**If scaling beyond trusted network (Phase 1+):**

Disputes will happen. How to handle?

**Option A: Build mediation system** (rejected)
- Requires human mediators (becomes a service)
- Triggers regulatory scrutiny
- Expensive (need staff, training, processes)

**Option B: Social media transparency** (selected)
- Parties post evidence publicly (X, Instagram, TikTok with #AsgayaDispute)
- Asgaya app searches and displays links to posts
- Users review evidence and decide for themselves
- Asgaya just aggregates public information (like Google Search)

**Why Option B works:**
- No Asgaya judgment (just linking to public info)
- No moderation (social platforms handle that)
- No liability (we're not hosting dispute content)
- Information society service stays intact ✅

**Implementation (Phase 1+):**
```
Asgaya app searches: "#AsgayaDispute Bodega Centro 0x789abc..."
Results: 2 posts found on X.com
App displays: "2 disputes found. [View on X →]"
User clicks, reads evidence, decides whether to trust merchant.
```

**Reputation emerges organically:**
- Merchants with multiple credible disputes → Avoided naturally
- Merchants with clean history → Trusted
- False accusations without evidence → Ignored by community

---

RISK SCENARIOS
--------------

### Scenario 1: Recipient Takes Cash and Runs

**Flow:**
1. Merchant hands 500,000 VES to Elena
2. Elena runs without signing
3. Covenant times out (24h)
4. Split refund: Merchant portion → Iris, Seller portion → Seller

**Who loses:** Merchant (lost 500,000 VES)

**Who's responsible:** Merchant (retail theft risk)

**Mitigation (Phase 0):**
- Only accept trusted recipients (sender vouches)
- Merchant knows recipient personally (family/friend)
- Expected: Zero incidents in Phase 0

**Mitigation (Phase 1+):**
- Merchants check recipient's history (past transactions)
- Refuse unknown/suspicious recipients
- Same as retail: assess customer, manage risk

**Not Asgaya's problem:** This is physical theft, not protocol failure.

### Scenario 2: Merchant Tricks Recipient into Signing

**Flow:**
1. Merchant: "Tap here to confirm" (before giving cash)
2. Elena signs (doesn't realize)
3. Merchant signs too → Covenant executes
4. Merchant receives BCH
5. Merchant never gives cash

**Who loses:** Elena (got no cash, but covenant executed)

**Who's responsible:** Partially Asgaya (UX), partially Elena (vigilance)

**Mitigation (Phase 0):**
- Only trusted merchants (sender vouches)
- Elena knows merchant personally (family/friend)
- Expected: Zero incidents in Phase 0

**Mitigation (Phase 1+):**
- **UX warnings:** "DON'T SIGN UNTIL CASH IS IN YOUR HAND"
- **Education:** Video tutorials, in-app prompts
- **Social proof:** Check merchant's history before accepting

**Why covenant can't prevent:**
Covenant sees cryptographic signatures (both valid). Can't detect if Elena was tricked.
This is social engineering, requires human vigilance.

### Scenario 3: Honest Disagreement

**Flow:**
1. Merchant: "I gave 500,000, she won't sign"
2. Elena: "He gave 450,000, not enough"
3. Both refuse to sign
4. Timeout → Split refund

**Who loses:** Both (deal didn't complete)

**Who's responsible:** Ambiguous (no way to tell who's right)

**Covenant behavior:**
- Doesn't judge who's right
- Just refunds after timeout
- Iris gets €99.50 BCH back
- Seller keeps €100 Bizum + €7.50 BCH

**Phase 1+ handling:**
- Both parties can post evidence on social media
- Future users review and decide
- If merchant has pattern of "recipient won't sign" → Avoided
- If recipient has pattern of "merchant shorted me" → Not trusted

---

TRADE-OFFS ACCEPTED
-------------------

**No disputes resolved in Phase 0:**
- If dispute occurs → Manual intervention by Asgaya team
- No formal process (not needed for 50-100 trusted transactions)

**No instant justice:**
- If merchant scams recipient → Elena loses, covenant can't revert
- If recipient scams merchant → Merchant loses, no recourse via covenant
- Physical world enforcement (police, social pressure) is only option

**Reputation is slow:**
- Takes multiple transactions for patterns to emerge
- Bad actors can scam once before caught
- But: Trusted network in Phase 0 minimizes this risk

**Social media is imperfect:**
- False accusations possible (no verification)
- Requires users to research (friction)
- Privacy concerns (disputes are public)

**But: These trade-offs preserve regulatory compliance.**

Alternative (formal mediation) would trigger licensing.

---

COMPARISON TO ESCROW MODEL
--------------------------

**Old escrow model:**
- Mediator investigates disputes
- 3-strike merchant system
- Evidence collection (video, GPS, witnesses)
- Mediator decides who's right
- Funds distributed based on judgment

**Problem:** Mediator becomes regulated intermediary.

**New covenant model:**
- No mediator
- Covenant executes or refunds autonomously
- No investigation, no judgment
- Social media for reputation (optional)

**Benefit:** Regulatory compliance maintained.

**Cost:** Less "justice" for individual disputes.

**Net: Compliance > individual dispute resolution.**

FUTURE ENHANCEMENTS
-------------------

**Phase 1: Social Media Transparency**
- App searches #AsgayaDispute hashtag
- Displays count: "2 disputes found for this merchant"
- Links to X/Instagram posts
- Users review evidence before accepting bounty

**Phase 2: On-Chain Reputation**
- Successful transactions recorded on-chain
- "Merchant #4729: 247 successful transactions, 2 disputes"
- Reputation tokens (non-transferable)
- Algorithmic trust scores

**Phase 3: Decentralized Insurance**
- Merchants can buy insurance (stake extra BCH)
- If scam proven, insurance pays out
- Community pools fund insurance
- Still no central mediator (autonomous smart contract insurance)

**But Phase 0: Keep it simple. Trusted parties. Zero expected disputes.**

==============================================
3.4 EXCHANGE RATE HANDLING
==============================================

**Decision Date:** May 11, 2026 (Updated for covenant architecture)
**Status:** Three-layer model (display, protocol, local)
**Category:** User Experience

---

THE DECISION
------------

**Exchange rates are for UX display only. Protocol operates in BCH.**

**Three-layer architecture:**

1. **Layer 1: Sender UX (EUR → VES display)**
   - Iris sees: "Send €100 → Elena receives ~500,000 VES"
   - Uses: EUR/USD rate + USD/VES rate (DolarAPI)
   - Purpose: Help sender understand value being sent

2. **Layer 2: BCH Protocol (actual settlement)**
   - Covenant specified: €100 worth of BCH (at maturity rate)
   - Covenant distributes: BCH amounts (0.0995 to merchant, 0.0075 to seller)
   - No EUR or VES involved in covenant (just BCH)

3. **Layer 3: Recipient UX (BCH → VES cash out)**
   - Elena sees: "Claim 500,000 VES at merchant"
   - Merchant sees: "Hand 500,000 VES, receive 0.0995 BCH"
   - Uses: Current BCH/VES rate (calculated from BCH/USD + USD/VES)

**Key: Protocol only cares about BCH. Exchange rates are UX convenience.**

---

RATIONALE
---------

### Why Protocol Doesn't Touch Fiat

**If covenant specified EUR amounts:**
- How would blockchain know EUR/BCH rate? (oracle problem)
- Who decides "correct" rate? (centralization)
- What if oracle fails? (single point of failure)

**By specifying BCH amounts:**
- Blockchain only handles BCH (native asset)
- No oracle needed for covenant execution
- Rate calculation happens off-chain (in apps)
- Covenant just enforces: "If both sign, distribute these BCH amounts"

**EUR-denominated promise is implemented via:**
- App calculates: €99.50 / current_BCH_price = X BCH
- Covenant distributes: X BCH to merchant
- Merchant receives: €99.50 worth at execution time

### Why Three Layers

**Sender thinks in EUR/VES:**
- "I want to send €100 and have Elena receive 500,000 VES"
- Not: "I want to send 0.1 BCH and hope it's worth 500,000 VES"
- EUR/VES display helps sender make decisions

**Protocol operates in BCH:**
- Blockchain only understands BCH
- Covenant can't hold EUR or VES (not on-chain)
- BCH is the bridge currency

**Recipient thinks in VES:**
- "I'm receiving 500,000 VES cash"
- Not: "Merchant is giving me 0.0995 BCH worth 500,000 VES"
- VES display helps recipient understand value

**Each layer sees what matters to them.**

---

RATE SOURCES
------------

### EUR/VES Display Rate (Layer 1)

**Source: DolarAPI** (Venezuelan parallel market rate)
- API: `https://dolarapi.com/v1/dolares`
- Returns: USD/VES blue dollar rate (real market, not official)
- Example: 1 USD = 50,000 VES (vs. 36.5 VES official rate)

**Why DolarAPI:**
- Most accurate reflection of actual Venezuelan exchange market
- Updated frequently (multiple times per day)
- Free public API
- Widely used in Venezuela for real-time rates

**EUR/VES calculated:**
```
EUR/USD rate: From ECB or forex API (e.g., 1 EUR = 1.08 USD)
USD/VES rate: From DolarAPI (e.g., 1 USD = 50,000 VES)
EUR/VES rate: 1 EUR × 1.08 USD/EUR × 50,000 VES/USD = 54,000 VES
```

**Cache strategy:**
- DolarAPI: Cache for 5 minutes (updates frequently)
- EUR/USD: Cache for 1 minute (forex markets)
- Recalculate at covenant creation (fresh rate for sender)

### BCH/EUR Spot Rate (Layer 2)

**Source: CoinGecko or Kraken API**
- Primary: CoinGecko `https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/simple/price?ids=bitcoin-cash&vs_currencies=eur`
- Fallback: Kraken BCH/EUR trading pair
- Returns: Current BCH spot price in EUR

**Used at:**
- Covenant creation (estimate BCH needed)
- Covenant maturity (calculate exact BCH to distribute)

**Cache strategy:**
- Cache for 1 minute (crypto prices move fast)
- Fetch fresh at moment of covenant execution

### BCH/VES Local Rate (Layer 3)

**Calculated from BCH/USD + USD/VES:**
```
BCH/USD: From CoinGecko (e.g., 1 BCH = $500 USD)
USD/VES: From DolarAPI (e.g., 1 USD = 50,000 VES)
BCH/VES: 1 BCH × $500 × 50,000 VES/USD = 25,000,000 VES
```

**Merchant sees:**
- "Hand 500,000 VES, receive 0.0995 BCH (~5,025,000 VES at current rate)"
- Profit: 25,000 VES (0.5% fee)

---

VOLATILITY DURING RATE WINDOWS
-------------------------------

**Problem:** What if rates change between display and execution?

**Example scenario:**
```
Covenant creation:
├─ BCH/EUR: €1,000 per BCH
├─ EUR/VES: 54,000 VES per EUR
├─ Display: "Send €100 → Elena gets 5,400,000 VES"

Covenant execution (12h later):
├─ BCH/EUR: €1,020 per BCH (BCH rose 2%)
├─ EUR/VES: 54,500 VES per EUR (VES depreciated 1%)
├─ Reality: Elena gets 5,450 VES per EUR = 5,450,000 VES
```

**Elena got MORE than displayed!** (BCH rose, overcollateralization surplus)

**Opposite scenario:**
```
Covenant execution:
├─ BCH/EUR: €980 per BCH (BCH dropped 2%)
├─ EUR/VES: 53,500 VES per EUR (VES strengthened 1%)
├─ Reality: Elena gets 5,350 VES per EUR = 5,350,000 VES
```

**Elena got LESS than displayed.** (BCH dropped, overcollateralization absorbed it)

**How overcollateralization protects:**
- Seller posted €107 worth (7% buffer)
- Even after 2% drop: Still enough to give Elena promised amount
- Seller absorbs the volatility (that's what 0.5% fee is for)

**±1% variation is acceptable:**
- DolarAPI rate can move ±1% in 24h (Venezuelan inflation)
- BCH can move ±2-3% in 24h (typical crypto volatility)
- Combined: ±3-4% total variation possible
- But 7% overcollateralization covers this
- Elena gets 96-104% of displayed amount (acceptable for Phase 0)

**Phase 0 will validate:** Are variations too large? Need to adjust buffer?

---

RATE TRANSPARENCY
-----------------

**All rates are publicly verifiable:**

**Sender can verify:**
- EUR/VES rate: Check DolarAPI themselves (`dolarapi.com`)
- BCH/EUR rate: Check CoinGecko or Kraken
- Compare Asgaya display to public sources
- If mismatch: Don't create covenant

**Recipient can verify:**
- VES amount promised: Check covenant details
- Current BCH/VES rate: Check CoinGecko + DolarAPI
- If merchant offers less: Refuse to sign

**Merchant can verify:**
- BCH amount they'll receive: Check covenant on-chain
- Current BCH/VES value: Check CoinGecko + DolarAPI
- If not profitable: Don't accept bounty

**No trust needed:** All rates come from public, independent sources.

---

COMPARISON TO OLD MODEL
-----------------------

**Old escrow model:**
- Escrow buys BCH on Kraken after merchant confirms
- Rate locked: Kraken market rate at purchase time
- Fees: Kraken trading fee (~0.26%) deducted first
- Trust: Escrow chooses exchange, users trust their judgment

**New covenant model:**
- BCH seller already owns BCH (no purchase at settlement)
- Rate: Spot market rate at covenant maturity
- Fees: No exchange fee (seller's BCH, not buying)
- Trust: Public APIs (CoinGecko, DolarAPI), anyone can verify

**Benefit of new model:**
- No exchange dependency (Kraken outage doesn't break Asgaya)
- No exchange fees (0.26% saved)
- Transparent rates (public APIs)
- Faster execution (no waiting for Kraken trade to confirm)

FUTURE ENHANCEMENTS
-------------------

**Phase 1: Rate History**
- Show sender: "Average rate last 24h: 53,800 VES/EUR (yours: 54,000)"
- Help sender decide if current rate is good

**Phase 2: Rate Alerts**
- Sender sets: "Notify me when EUR/VES > 55,000"
- App sends push notification when threshold crossed
- Sender creates covenant at favorable rate

**Phase 3: Rate Guarantees**
- Sender locks rate at covenant creation
- Even if rate moves, Elena gets exact promised amount
- Requires larger overcollateralization (10-15%)
- Optional feature for risk-averse senders

**But Phase 0: Keep simple. Display rates, overcollateralization handles volatility.**

==============================================

**Language:** CashScript (high-level BCH contract language)
**Compiler:** CashScript compiler → Bitcoin Script

---

CONTRACT STRUCTURE
------------------

```cashscript
pragma cashscript ^0.8.0;

contract AsgayaRemittanceCovenant(
    // Party public keys
    pubkey merchantPubKey,
    pubkey recipientPubKey,
    pubkey sellerPubKey,
    pubkey senderRefundPubKey,
    
    // Payment amounts (in satoshis, calculated at creation)
    // Note: These are BCH amounts, not EUR
    // EUR promise is implemented by calculating BCH amount at maturity rate
    int merchantAmount,      // ~€99.50 worth at maturity rate
    int sellerAmount,        // ~€7.50 worth (overcollateral + fee)
    
    // Timeout (Unix timestamp, 24 hours from creation)
    int timeout
) {
    // Function 1: Normal execution (both parties co-sign)
    function mature(sig merchantSig, sig recipientSig) {
        // Verify both signatures are valid
        require(checkSig(merchantSig, merchantPubKey));
        require(checkSig(recipientSig, recipientPubKey));
        
        // Construct outputs:
        // Output 1: merchantAmount → merchant address (P2PKH)
        // Output 2: sellerAmount → seller address (P2PKH)
        
        bytes20 merchantPKH = hash160(merchantPubKey);
        bytes20 sellerPKH = hash160(sellerPubKey);
        
        bytes8 merchantOutput = bytes8(merchantAmount);
        bytes8 sellerOutput = bytes8(sellerAmount);
        
        // OP_RETURN commitment (optional: reference bounty ID)
        bytes opReturn = new OutputNullData([
            0x41, 0x53, 0x47, 0x41, 0x59, 0x41  // "ASGAYA"
        ]);
        
        // Verify transaction structure matches expected outputs
        require(tx.hashOutputs == hash256(
            merchantOutput + merchantPKH +
            sellerOutput + sellerPKH +
            opReturn
        ));
    }
    
    // Function 2: Timeout refund (after 24h, split refund)
    function refundTimeout() {
        // Require timeout has expired
        require(tx.time >= timeout);
        
        // Split refund:
        // Output 1: merchantAmount → sender refund address
        //           (Iris gets her money back minus merchant fee)
        // Output 2: sellerAmount → seller address
        //           (Seller keeps overcollateralization + fee)
        
        bytes20 senderPKH = hash160(senderRefundPubKey);
        bytes20 sellerPKH = hash160(sellerPubKey);
        
        bytes8 senderRefund = bytes8(merchantAmount);
        bytes8 sellerRefund = bytes8(sellerAmount);
        
        require(tx.hashOutputs == hash256(
            senderRefund + senderPKH +
            sellerRefund + sellerPKH
        ));
    }
    
    // Function 3: Early cancellation (seller cancels if no Bizum)
    function cancelNoBizum(sig sellerSig) {
        // Verify seller signature
        require(checkSig(sellerSig, sellerPubKey));
        
        // Only allow within first 5 minutes (before Bizum window expires)
        require(tx.time <= timeout - 23 hours 55 minutes);
        
        // Full refund to seller (all BCH back)
        bytes20 sellerPKH = hash160(sellerPubKey);
        bytes8 fullAmount = bytes8(merchantAmount + sellerAmount);
        
        require(tx.hashOutputs == hash256(
            fullAmount + sellerPKH
        ));
    }
}
```

---

KEY DESIGN DECISIONS
--------------------

### 1. EUR-Denominated via Off-Chain Calculation

**Problem:** Blockchain doesn't know EUR/BCH rate.

**Solution:** App calculates BCH amounts at maturity:
```
Covenant creation:
├─ Iris specifies: €100 worth
├─ App estimates: ~0.1 BCH needed (rough estimate)
├─ Seller posts: 0.107 BCH (7% buffer)

Covenant maturity:
├─ Current BCH/EUR rate: €1,020/BCH
├─ App calculates: €99.50 / €1,020 = 0.09755 BCH for merchant
├─ Remaining: 0.107 - 0.09755 = 0.00945 BCH for seller
├─ Transaction specifies these amounts
├─ Covenant just enforces: "If both sign, distribute as specified"
```

**Covenant doesn't fetch prices.** It just validates signatures and distributes amounts.

### 2. Three Execution Paths

**Path 1: mature()** - Normal happy path
- Both merchant and recipient sign
- Covenant distributes BCH as promised
- 95%+ of transactions (Phase 0 goal)

**Path 2: refundTimeout()** - Elena never claims
- 24 hours pass, no signatures
- Automatic split refund
- Iris gets most money back, seller keeps fee

**Path 3: cancelNoBizum()** - Iris doesn't pay
- Seller posted BCH, but no Bizum received
- Seller can reclaim BCH within 5 min
- Protects seller from capital lockup

**No "dispute resolution" path.** Covenant is autonomous.

### 3. Public Key Authentication

**All parties identified by public keys:**
- Merchant: Generated when merchant creates wallet
- Recipient: Generated when Elena creates wallet (or uses ephemeral key)
- Seller: Seller's main BCH address
- Sender refund: Iris's refund address

**Security:**
- Private keys never leave devices
- Signatures generated locally
- No central key management

12 PARAMETERS TO VALIDATE
--------------------------

### 1. Fee Split (0.5% each)

**Hypothesis:** 0.5% seller + 0.5% merchant attracts participants

**Metrics:**
- Seller acceptance rate (target: >90% of bounties accepted within 5 min)
- Merchant acceptance rate (target: >80% of merchants willing to onboard)
- Participant feedback: "Is 0.5% worth the effort?"

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If seller acceptance <70%: Increase to 0.6% seller, 0.4% merchant
- If merchant recruitment slow: Increase to 0.4% seller, 0.6% merchant

### 2. Overcollateralization Rate (7%)

**Hypothesis:** 7% buffer covers 95%+ of 24h volatility

**Metrics:**
- Margin call frequency (target: <5% of transactions)
- Price movements during covenant lifetime (track max ±% change)
- Seller losses due to overcollateralization

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If margin calls >10%: Increase to 10% or 15%
- If price drops >7% in >10% of covenants: Increase buffer
- If never any margin calls after 100 transactions: Consider reducing to 5%

### 3. Covenant Timeout (24h)

**Hypothesis:** 24h gives Elena flexibility without excessive capital lockup

**Metrics:**
- Average claim time (how long until Elena actually claims)
- Timeout frequency (how often does 24h expire)
- Seller feedback: "Is 24h too long for capital lockup?"

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If avg claim time <2h: Consider reducing timeout to 12h
- If timeouts >20%: Investigate why (Elena forgot? Coordination issue?)
- If sellers complain about lockup: Reduce timeout or increase fee

### 4. Bizum Window (5 min)

**Hypothesis:** 5 min is enough for sender to open Bizum and pay

**Metrics:**
- Cancellation rate (how often does 5 min expire)
- Sender feedback: "Was 5 min enough time?"
- Average time between notification and Bizum sent

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If cancellations >10%: Increase to 10 min
- If senders say "too rushed": Increase window
- If never any cancellations: Consider keeping at 5 min (working well)

### 5. Margin Call Response (60 min)

**Hypothesis:** Sellers can respond to margin calls within 60 min

**Metrics:**
- Margin call response rate (how often do sellers add BCH vs. let refund)
- Response time (average time to add BCH)
- Seller feedback: "Was 60 min enough?"

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If response rate <50%: Increase to 120 min or improve notification
- If sellers always respond quickly: Keep at 60 min
- If margin calls cause too many failed transactions: Increase overcollateralization instead

### 6. Bounty Code Length (4 digits)

**Hypothesis:** 4 digits is memorable and secure (10,000 combinations)

**Metrics:**
- User feedback: "Easy to remember and tell merchant?"
- Collision rate (same code assigned twice in short time)
- Fraud attempts (someone guessing codes)

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If users say "hard to remember": Add word-based codes ("apple-7293")
- If collisions occur: Increase to 5-6 digits
- If fraud attempts: Add rate limiting or CAPTCHA

### 7. Transaction Sizes (€50-€500 suggested)

**Hypothesis:** Most remittances are €100-€200, with €50-€500 range

**Metrics:**
- Distribution of transaction sizes (histogram)
- Requests for larger/smaller amounts
- Feedback: "Would you send more if limit higher?"

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If many requests for >€500: Allow larger (but increase overcollateralization)
- If many requests for <€50: Allow smaller (but check if fees too high)
- If avg is €100-150: Optimize UX for that range (defaults, presets)

### 8. Processing Fee (€0.10 for expired covenants)

**Hypothesis:** €0.10 covers gas costs for covenant deployment

**Metrics:**
- Actual gas costs (average BCH transaction fee)
- Timeout frequency (how often do we deploy covenants that never mature)
- Sender willingness to pay €0.10 upfront

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If gas costs >€0.10: Increase processing fee
- If gas costs <€0.05: Decrease processing fee
- If timeouts rare (<5%): Consider refunding processing fee

### 9. Notification Timing (Instant SMS + Push)

**Hypothesis:** SMS + Push notifications reach users effectively

**Metrics:**
- Delivery rate (% of notifications delivered)
- Time to open app after notification
- Missed claim rate (did notification fail to reach Elena?)

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If delivery <95%: Add email backup or improve SMS gateway
- If users don't see notifications: Add sound/vibration alerts
- If delayed delivery: Investigate Twilio/FCM latency

### 10. Geographic Variations (Urban vs. Rural)

**Hypothesis:** Urban and rural areas have different needs

**Metrics:**
- Claim time by location (urban faster or slower?)
- Merchant density (merchants per 10km radius)
- Infrastructure issues (power outages, connectivity in rural areas)

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If rural claim times >>urban: Increase timeout for rural bounties
- If rural has fewer merchants: Focus recruitment there
- If connectivity issues: Add offline mode or SMS-only flow

### 11. Market Conditions (Volatile vs. Calm)

**Hypothesis:** Calm markets need less overcollateralization

**Metrics:**
- BCH volatility during covenant lifetime (±% change)
- Correlation: margin calls vs. market volatility
- Seller behavior: accept more bounties when calm, fewer when volatile

**Adjustment criteria:**
- If high volatility causes problems: Dynamic overcollateralization
- If sellers avoid volatile periods: Increase buffer or incentives
- If calm markets work perfectly: Consider reducing buffer

### 12. Corridor-Specific Parameters

**Hypothesis:** Spain→Venezuela needs same parameters as other corridors

**Metrics:**
- (Phase 0 is single corridor, can't validate yet)
- Note differences for future: EUR/VES vs. USD/MXN behavior
- Inflation rates affect timeout (Venezuelan bolívar depreciates daily)

**Adjustment criteria:**
- Phase 1: Test second corridor (US→Mexico)
- Compare metrics: Same fee split? Same timeout?
- If different: Implement corridor-specific parameter tables

SECTION 6: GLOSSARY
==============================================

Key terms and definitions (condensed for AI consumption).

**Asgaya:** Permissionless BCH remittance protocol using covenant smart contracts

**BCH (Bitcoin Cash):** Cryptocurrency used as bridge between fiat systems

**BCH Seller:** Entity that posts overcollateralized BCH to covenant, earns 0.5% fee

**Bizum:** Spanish mobile payment system (instant P2P fiat transfers)

**Bounty:** Open covenant published to bulletin board, accepting offers from sellers

**Bounty Code:** 4-digit code (e.g., 8473) linking recipient to specific covenant

**Bulletin Board:** Asgaya's coordination platform showing available bounties

**CashScript:** High-level language for BCH smart contracts

**Co-signing:** Both merchant and recipient cryptographically sign covenant to execute

**Covenant:** BCH smart contract holding overcollateralized BCH, executes autonomously

**DolarAPI:** Venezuelan API providing real parallel market USD/VES exchange rates

**EUR-Denominated Promise:** Covenant promises fixed EUR value, delivered in BCH

**Margin Call:** Alert when BCH drops >7%, seller must add BCH or let refund

**Merchant:** Local business that hands VES cash to recipient, receives BCH reward

**MiCA:** EU Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation (governs crypto services)

**Overcollateralization:** Seller posts 107% of needed BCH (7% buffer for volatility)

**PagoMóvil:** Venezuelan mobile payment system (bank transfer equivalent)

**Pull System:** Recipient triggers execution when ready (vs. push = sender triggers)

**Recipient:** Person receiving money (Elena in Venezuela)

**Sender:** Person sending money (Iris in Spain)

**Split Refund:** Timeout distributes merchant portion to sender, seller portion to seller

**Timeout:** 24h window after which covenant auto-refunds if not executed

**VES (Venezuelan Bolívar):** Local currency in Venezuela (hyperinflationary)
