The fiat → BCH flow · recorded demo

Send money home
in the money they use.

The sender pays in fiat (Bizum, in Spain). The merchant's own device turns it into BCH. Mercar never holds the money.

Recorded on testnet3 (research), before BLAZE, with three Asgaya devices: the merchant, the recipient and the sender. The BCH settlement is the next step to move to mainnet; the board, the identity anchor and the chat you can use today are on mainnet.

The four steps

  1. The sender pays in fiat. A Bizum (or any instant rail) to the merchant, with the concept the merchant's device gave them.
  2. The merchant's phone sees the payment. The Asgaya app reads the bank notification on the merchant's own device.
  3. The merchant's device sends the BCH. It funds the covenant (or pays the recipient directly) from the merchant's wallet. No custodian, no platform in the middle.
  4. The recipient gets BCH. The BCH arrives in the recipient's wallet; the fiat never touched a crypto exchange.

Why Bizum

Bizum is the rail most people already use in Spain: instant, familiar, and free for the payer. Mercar treats it as one rail, not the only one: the same discovery and coordination layer can carry SEPA Instant, Pix or any other instant fiat rail. The protocol is open; the merchant's device is the source of the payment instructions.
